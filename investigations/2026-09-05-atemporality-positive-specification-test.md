# Atemporality Positive-Specification Test

**Date:** 2026-09-05  
**Status:** Complete at this level  
**Verdict:** Boundary

## First separation

[
oxed{	ext{positive atemporal specification} 
eq 	ext{actual atemporal instantiation}}
]

This test concerns only the first term.

No candidate is required to exist, occur, be instantiated, or be exercised. Requiring any of those would import (O_1) before the predicate under test had been specified.

---

## Object

> **Can “atemporal” name a positively specifiable kind of structure, or does it only mean “temporal, with a minus sign”?**

The test is deliberately narrower than the neighboring investigations.

## Non-objects

This file does **not** ask:

- whether any atemporal structure is actual;
- whether the Cut generates time;
- what (F) is;
- whether verb-first ontology succeeds;
- whether act explains actuality;
- what makes totality one.

No result here transfers automatically to those questions.

---

## Candidate-form rule

The required form is:

> **An atemporal structure is ______.**

The blank must use only vocabulary already licensed as Derived or Regulative.

It may not use:

- before;
- after;
- then;
- happens;
- becomes;
- does;
- passage;
- succession;
- performance;
- exercise;

nor:

- timeless;
- non-temporal;
- outside time;
- without time;

nor the foil vocabulary:

- static;
- frozen;
- unchanging;
- fixed;
- simultaneous;
- all at once.

The final group is excluded because it specifies its target by contrast with change rather than positively.

---

## Leak protocol

Every candidate is tested in this order.

### (O_2): passage

Does the candidate remain intelligible if passage is unavailable as a foil?

### (O_1): token existence

Does the candidate require a concrete token or actual instantiation in order to be what it says it is?

### Exercise

Does the candidate require anything to be carried out?

A candidate that survives all three checks still must specify **atemporality**, rather than merely some positive structural or descriptive condition compatible with atemporality.

# Test I — Complete relational structure

Candidate:

> **An atemporal structure is a complete relational structure.**

### (O_2)

“Relational” survives without passage.

“Complete” is less secure. It can mean extensionally specified, but ordinary completion language also invites a contrast with an unfinished process. Under the strongest structural reading, that contrast can be removed.

So (O_2) does not immediately eliminate the candidate.

### (O_1)

No token existence is required. An abstract structure can satisfy the sentence.

### Exercise

No exercise is required.

### Failure

The surviving content is:

[
	ext{structurally complete}
]

not:

[
	ext{atemporal}.
]

Nothing in relational completeness excludes temporal structure. A temporally ordered structure can be complete in exactly the same structural sense.

**Verdict:** near-miss. Positive structural specification survives, but it does not specify atemporality.

---

# Test II — Extensional totality of relations

Candidate:

> **An atemporal structure is an extensional totality of relations.**

### (O_2)

Under an extensional reading, the sentence can be understood without passage.

### (O_1)

No concrete token is required.

### Exercise

No exercise is required.

### Failure

The sentence identifies a way of presenting or collecting relational structure. It does not identify a temporal condition.

A temporal structure can also be given as an extensional totality of relations.

The candidate therefore specifies:

[
	ext{a kind of structural description}
]

rather than:

[
	ext{atemporality}.
]

**Verdict:** near-miss.

---

# Test III — Automorphism-invariant structural facts

Candidate:

> **An atemporal structure is a structure exhaustively characterized by automorphism-invariant facts.**

### (O_2)

The structural vocabulary survives without passage.

### (O_1)

No token existence is required.

### Exercise

No exercise is required.

### Failure

This is the strongest positive candidate because automorphism facts are independently licensed structural vocabulary.

But the candidate does not specify atemporality.

A temporal structure may have automorphism-invariant facts. Restricting a description to such facts does not remove temporal relations from the ontology; at most it constrains what the description says about the structure.

This is the important near-miss anticipated by the protocol:

[
oxed{	ext{this specifies a restricted descriptive vocabulary, not atemporality}.}
]

[

egoperatorname{Transitive}(operatorname{Aut}(S))
]

and related automorphism facts therefore cannot be promoted to atemporality merely because they are positive and clock-free.

**Verdict:** near-miss.

# Test IV — Internal-relational exhaustiveness

Candidate:

> **An atemporal structure is a structure whose identity conditions are exhausted by internal relations.**

### (O_2)

The candidate can be stated without passage.

### (O_1)

No concrete token is required.

### Exercise

No exercise is required.

### Failure

Again, the sentence specifies a structural dependence condition. It does not distinguish temporal from non-temporal structure.

Temporal relations can themselves be internal relations.

The candidate therefore leaves the target untouched.

**Verdict:** target shift.

---

# Test V — Mathematical structure

Candidate:

> **An atemporal structure is a mathematical structure specified by its domain and relations.**

### (O_2)

The candidate survives without passage.

### (O_1)

No actual instantiation is required.

### Exercise

No exercise is required.

### Failure

“Mathematical” does not entail atemporal. Mathematical structures can represent temporal order, succession, and change.

The candidate gives an abstractness or representational category, not a positive specification of atemporality.

**Verdict:** target shift.

---

# Consolidated result

Every tested candidate succeeded at one thing:

[
	ext{positive structural specification without passage vocabulary}.
]

None succeeded at the additional thing required:

[
	ext{showing that the specification is a specification of atemporality}.
]

The repeated failure has one precise form:

[
oxed{	ext{structural positivity} 
eq 	ext{atemporality}.}
]

The strongest survivors were descriptions restricted to structural relations, invariants, or extensional conditions. But every such candidate remained compatible with temporal structure.

Conversely, every obvious candidate that would exclude temporal structure did so by invoking passage, change, succession, or their absence as a foil, and was therefore excluded by the protocol.

## Verdict

[
oxed{	extbf{Boundary}}
]

No tested positive specification of “atemporal” survived as a specification of atemporality rather than as:

- structural completeness;
- abstract description;
- restricted descriptive vocabulary;
- internal relationality;
- or another positive condition compatible with temporal structure.

The important near-miss is retained:

> **A structure can be specified positively without temporal vocabulary without thereby being specified as atemporal.**

## Not earned

This test does **not** establish:

- that atemporality is impossible;
- that no positive specification can ever be found;
- that all structure is temporal;
- that the Cut generates time;
- that occurrence requires time;
- that actualization is therefore primitive;
- or that “it just can” is justified by an established atemporal side.

The bounded search stops here.

If later work invokes an “atemporal” side, it must not treat this test as having supplied one.
