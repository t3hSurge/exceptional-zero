# Diachronic Identity: Selective Common Structure — Usefulness Test

**Status:** Open investigation; selective branch tested abstractly against the joint AR-1 / AR-6 burden  
**Scope:** Whether a selective representation-independent common structure `G*` can be non-idle with respect to representation admissibility while leaving identity selection genuinely open  
**Depends on:** `diachronic-identity-representation-invariance.md`; Test A; Test B; fixed `J_cut`; no candidate `Σ` introduced

---

## 1. Question

The previous pass established that AR-1 (target independence) and AR-6 (no hidden `Σ`) are **jointly satisfiable in form**. That result did not exhibit a concrete `G*` and did not show that selective structure can do useful work.

The present test therefore asks the remaining question:

> **Can selective structure be independently bounded in a way that materially constrains representation admissibility while leaving identity selection genuinely open?**

The distinction between two kinds of usefulness is essential:

- **Representation-usefulness:** the selection changes which descriptions count as admissible representations, for independently structural reasons.
- **Identity-usefulness:** the selection constrains which later stages count as the same entity.

AR-6 forbids the second kind from being performed prematurely. The test therefore seeks the narrow possibility of the first without the second.

No candidate `G*` or `Σ` is promoted by this document. The purpose is to determine whether the live region is empty, identity-idle, or genuinely viable at the representation layer.

---

## 2. Operational Criterion

A selective `G*` counts as a **non-idle representation selector** only if all of the following hold:

1. **Independent membership rule:** inclusion/exclusion is specified without identity vocabulary or target-stage reference.
2. **Structural licensing:** the rule is justified by facts such as ontological type, causal direction, locality, conservation, topology, or formal graph structure rather than by desired persistence verdicts.
3. **Projection adequacy:** both process-based `G_P` and material-based `G_M` remain recoverable as legitimate projections where the underlying history contains both kinds of relation.
4. **Identity non-determination:** the retained structure does not by itself choose Ship A over Ship B, or otherwise settle the later `Σ` question.
5. **Nontrivial representation effect:** the rule excludes at least some genuinely different descriptions, so it is not a universal bucket.
6. **Refinement stability:** admissible refinement does not turn an allowed representation into a forbidden one merely by adding descriptive detail.
7. **Coarse-graining stability:** admissible abstraction does not alter membership merely by reducing descriptive resolution.
8. **Counterfeit neutrality:** the rule neither manufactures nor suppresses the genuine/counterfeit distinction merely because that distinction is wanted by the later identity theory.
9. **Junction neutrality:** the rule does not encode the identity-terminating status of branches or merges.
10. **Generalizability:** the rule is not tuned solely to Theseus.

Failure of (4) is an AR-6 failure. Failure of (5) is identity-idleness in the strong sense: the structure does no substantive representation work. Failure of (6)–(10) shows that the apparent structural rule is not stable enough to serve as the common representation layer.

## 3. Abstract Classes of Selective Exclusion

No individual candidate is being promoted. Instead, the possible sources of independently licensed selectivity are tested by class.

### 3.1 Formal / Typological Exclusion

Example form: retain relations of specified mathematical type; exclude annotations, metadata, or predicates of another formal type.

**AR-1:** Can pass. The rule can be stated entirely formally.

**AR-6:** Can pass if both process and material relations remain in the retained vocabulary. Nothing requires the formal type distinction to decide persistence.

**Non-idleness:** It can genuinely distinguish a causal graph from a description that merely annotates the graph. Thus it is not vacuous.

**Problem:** It may be too weak to constrain the representation distinction that motivated `G*`. If both competing causal vocabularies survive unchanged, the Theseus selection problem remains untouched.

**Result:** Formally viable; potentially representation-useful but identity-idle.

### 3.2 Causal-Direction Exclusion

Example form: retain directed causal relations and exclude symmetric descriptive associations.

**AR-1:** Structurally specifiable.

**AR-6:** Can pass if both process and material causation are directed causal relations.

**Non-idleness:** It distinguishes causal structure from non-causal association.

**Problem:** It does not choose between process and material continuity. If one of those is excluded merely because it is not classified as causal, the exclusion becomes identity-consequential and must be independently justified by the underlying causal ontology.

**Result:** Same pattern: genuine representation work is possible without identity selection, but the Theseus bottleneck survives intact.

### 3.3 Locality / Spacetime Exclusion

Example form: retain relations satisfying an independently defined locality or adjacency condition.

**AR-1:** Structurally available.

**AR-6:** Passes only where the locality condition leaves both process and material provenance available. If it eliminates one continuity family in the relevant case, the selection must be shown to follow from the structural ontology rather than from its identity consequence.

**Refinement pressure:** Finer descriptions may reveal local paths that a coarse description hides.

**Coarse-graining pressure:** A coarse edge may no longer display the locality facts that justified membership.

Therefore the rule requires an explicit preservation condition across changes of resolution.

**Result:** Live in principle, but representation stability is an additional burden rather than an automatic consequence.

### 3.4 Conservation / Invariant Exclusion

Example form: retain relations preserving a specified conserved quantity or structural invariant.

**AR-1:** Potentially strong: conservation can be independently specified.

**AR-6:** Does not automatically decide identity. Process and material continuity can both participate in histories satisfying the same invariant.

**Non-idleness:** The invariant can exclude physically incompatible descriptions.

**Problem:** A conserved quantity can become identity-consequential in some domains and identity-idle in others. If the theory retains it because it tracks persistence in the target cases, AR-1's justification layer is violated.

**Result:** Potentially genuine representation-level selectivity, but not a general identity selector.

### 3.5 Pure Graph-Structural Exclusion

Example form: retain edges satisfying specified degree, connectivity, path, or topology conditions.

**AR-1:** Cleanly target-independent.

**AR-6:** Usually passes if both competing causal relations are retained within the same graph structure.

**Non-idleness:** Can distinguish structurally malformed representations.

**Problem:** If the graph property is chosen specifically because it isolates a preferred persistence path, it becomes hidden `Σ`. If it is chosen without that purpose, its connection to the Theseus problem may be merely indirect.

**Result:** Viable as representation hygiene; no demonstrated identity-selection power.

## 4. Adversarial Battery

The class-level possibilities above are now tested against the decisive cases.

### Test A — Ship of Theseus

`G*` must admit both process and material descriptions as projections if both are genuine structural aspects of the same physical history.

If the selective rule excludes one family solely because it yields the unwanted identity verdict, AR-6 fails.

If it excludes neither, the later `Σ` still faces the original choice.

**Result:** The selective branch cannot solve Theseus merely by being selective. A non-identity structural filter leaves the positive selection problem intact.

### Test B — Refinement

Suppose a coarse process edge is refined into many local process steps plus explicit material transfers.

A valid selective rule must not change its classification merely because one representation exposes additional structure that was already implicit.

A rule that keeps only process edges may pass this test by fiat, but that is precisely the kind of feature-level privileging AR-6 is meant to detect.

A rule based on independently specified structural type can remain stable, but then it need not distinguish process from material.

**Result:** Refinement stability is compatible with selective representation filtering, but does not generate the missing identity selector.

### Test C — Coarse-Graining

Collapse a detailed replacement history into a single transition.

If the common structure loses the process/material distinction under coarse-graining, a later `Σ` may no longer have access to both readings. If it retains both as abstracted attributes, the selection remains open.

Thus a useful selective rule must specify what information coarse-graining is permitted to forget independently of identity.

**Result:** Stability is possible only with an independently specified information-loss rule. It does not itself choose persistence.

### Test D — Fission

A fine representation explicitly contains a branch point. A coarse representation may compress the branching interval.

If `G*` erases the branch because branching is inconvenient for identity, AR-6 fails.

If branching remains structural but is merely represented at different resolutions, the fixed `J_cut` rule can operate after reconstruction of the relevant graph structure.

**Result:** A selective representation layer can remain neutral toward the identity consequence of branching.

### Test E — Fusion

The same argument applies to convergence. A representation filter must not decide that one incoming line is the identity-bearing line.

**Result:** Representation selectivity can be junction-neutral; `J_cut` remains the separate partition rule.

### Test F — Perfect Counterfeit

The robust counterfeit result requires that the external splice remain recoverable in the common structure.

A representation rule that simply omits all provenance information would make the counterfeit indistinguishable at the later selection layer and would therefore be insufficient.

A rule that retains provenance because provenance is an independently specified causal relation can preserve the distinction without declaring which stage is genuine.

**Result:** A selective `G*` can preserve the robust counterfeit result without itself performing identity selection.

### Test G — Counterfeit-Separating Redescription

If a redescription makes the external splice explicit, admissibility must not manufacture the distinction. The distinction must already be present in the represented causal structure.

**Result:** This is compatible with selective `G*` provided admissibility is based on structural equivalence rather than on the desired genuine/counterfeit classification.

### Test H — Redundant Causation

Parallel sufficient causes must not be lost merely because a selective rule chooses one arbitrarily.

An independently specified equivalence for redundant edges can aggregate them without selecting an identity-bearing mechanism.

**Result:** No necessary conflict with AR-1 or AR-6.

### Test I — Irrelevant Side-Effects

This is the strongest test of the phrase "identity-idle."

A rule that excludes side-effects because they are *irrelevant to identity* violates AR-6.

A rule that excludes them because they are, for example, outside the declared causal-system boundary can pass AR-1 and AR-6 — but then the exclusion is justified by system-boundary structure, not by persistence.

That is genuine representation-usefulness, but it does not tell `Σ` which causal relations matter to identity.

**Result:** The distinction between representation-usefulness and identity-usefulness survives the adversarial test.

### Test J — Causal Counterfeit

Suppose a counterfeit matches the genuine continuation on all features retained by `G*` but differs on an excluded feature.

If the excluded feature is later needed to distinguish the histories, `G*` was insufficient.

If the excluded feature is not needed by any legitimate later `Σ`, that cannot be known by appealing to the identity verdict. The exclusion therefore needs an independent structural boundary.

**Result:** No contradiction is forced, but the burden shifts to proving that the excluded feature lies outside the structural object for reasons independent of identity.

## 5. The Joint Result

The complete pressure test produces an important asymmetry.

### Result 1 — Selective structure is not forced into identity selection

AR-1 and AR-6 remain jointly satisfiable after the usefulness battery. There are structurally describable forms of selectivity that can change representation admissibility without deciding whether Ship A or Ship B is the persisting ship.

Therefore the following stronger claim is **not earned**:

\[
\boxed{\text{Every nontrivial selective }G^*\text{ necessarily hides }\Sigma.}
\]

That would overstate the evidence.

### Result 2 — Selective structure does not solve the Theseus selection problem

The surviving forms of selectivity can filter representations while retaining both competing causal vocabularies. They therefore leave the actual positive choice between process and material continuity untouched.

So the following weaker claim **is earned**:

\[
\boxed{\text{Representation-useful selectivity need not be identity-useful selectivity.}}
\]

### Result 3 — The useful selective region is conditional

For a selective `G*` to become a substantive survivor, it needs an independently bounded structural rule that:

- excludes something genuinely different,
- remains stable under admissible refinement and coarse-graining,
- preserves both process and material descriptions where both are structurally present,
- preserves the robust counterfeit distinction,
- and does not become a disguised selector in cases where its exclusions affect persistence.

No concrete rule satisfying all of these conditions has yet been exhibited.

### Result 4 — A Boundary is not earned

The battery does **not** establish that every useful selective `G*` is impossible. Nor does it establish that selective `G*` must collapse into the exhaustive branch.

The strongest honest status is therefore:

> **Selective `G*` survives the joint AR-1 / AR-6 usefulness test abstractly, but no concrete, independently bounded selective `G*` has yet been demonstrated. Its established role is representation-level rather than identity-selecting.**

---

## 6. What Has Been Ruled Out

The test does establish several negative results.

1. **Identity-motivated exclusion is disqualified.** A rule cannot exclude process, matter, information, organization, or side-effects merely because doing so produces the desired persistence verdict.
2. **Representation-level selectivity is not automatically circular.** Structural filters can be nontrivial without deciding identity.
3. **Selectivity alone cannot discharge the Theseus problem.** If both competing continuities remain represented, the missing `Σ` remains missing.
4. **Erasing the competing continuity is not a solution.** If the exclusion settles the persistence verdict, AR-6 has been violated.
5. **Calling every excluded feature "irrelevant" is circular.** Relevance must not be defined by the identity result.
6. **Refinement/coarse-graining stability does not automatically confer identity relevance.** A representation can be invariant while remaining neutral on persistence.

---

## 7. Remaining Live Space

The selective branch is now narrowed to a precise region:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c}
\text{independently bounded structural selectivity}\\
\land\\
\text{representation-useful}\\
\land\\
\text{identity-neutral}
\end{array}}
\]

This region is not known to be empty.

It is also not yet inhabited by a demonstrated concrete `G*`.

That distinction matters. The investigation has moved from an abstract concern — whether AR-1 and AR-6 conflict — to an existence question:

> **Does there exist a concrete, independently bounded structural boundary that has genuine representation-theoretic force while remaining neutral among competing identity-relevant continuities?**

Only an existence proof should promote a selective `G*` from conditional survivor to demonstrated survivor.
