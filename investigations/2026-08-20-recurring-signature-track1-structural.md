# Investigation: Recurring Signature — Track 1 (Structural Derivation)

**Date:** 2026-08-20  
**Status:** Open / original universal candidate refuted; narrower structural questions remain  
**Related canonical claim:** Recurring Signature (Part Eight) remains **Open**

---

## 1. Question

Can the recurring failure of third-person relational accounts (underdetermination or circularity) across for-this, truth, and diachronic identity be derived from the Exceptional Zero / *Z(S)*, or from a controlled generalization of non-transitivity, without inserting the target by hand?

## 2. Original candidate formulation

The original candidate was:

> Any structure *S* whose relations are restricted to a non-identity, third-person vocabulary cannot isolate the target (genuine past / for-this / truth-maker) by automorphism-invariant means without either leaving it underdetermined among orbits or requiring a relation that already encodes the target.

That universal formulation is **no longer tenable as stated** because the causal-provenance / Unique Causal Lineage (UCL) search produced a provisional survivor: an independently specified external causal relation that can distinguish genuine from counterfeit history without inserting an identity predicate.

The narrower structural theorem underneath the original candidate remains intact.

---

## 3. What would count against the narrower result

- A failure of the automorphism argument for a deliberately constructed perfect relational copy.
- A valid automorphism-invariant predicate that distinguishes two elements lying in the same orbit.
- A counterexample to the claim that exact relational isomorphism blocks intrinsic distinction.

The UCL result does **not** attack any of these. It attacks the stronger claim that every relevant third-person vocabulary can be forced into an exact-copy situation.

---

## 4. Method constraints

- Independent specification of any candidate distinguishing relation before testing.
- Circularity and target-shift checks at every step.
- Language-dependence must be tracked explicitly.
- No status upgrade by resemblance alone.
- Distinguish a theorem about a fixed signature from a universal theorem over classes of signatures.

---

## 5. Secure result: the perfect-copy theorem

Let

\[
S=(G\cup F,R)
\]

contain a genuine chain G and a rival chain F that are exact relational copies with respect to the complete permitted signature R.

If swapping corresponding elements preserves every relation in R, there is an automorphism \(\varphi\) with

\[
\varphi(g_i)=f_i.
\]

Therefore every automorphism-invariant property satisfies

\[
P(g_i)\iff P(f_i).
\]

So no structural predicate of R can distinguish the two.

This is a direct application of the original Exceptional Zero theorem. It is **Derived** from the established automorphism result.

The theorem remains fully intact.

---

## 6. What the UCL survivor changes

The original universalization implicitly treated “non-identity third-person vocabulary” as though it guaranteed copyability.

UCL shows that this is false unless the admissible class is narrowed.

UCL adds an independently specified external causal-provenance relation. In the tested construction, genuine and counterfeit histories share internal temporal, memory, informational, and ordinary causal structure but differ in their external causal origins.

Then the combined structure need not admit an automorphism exchanging them.

Thus:

\[
\text{non-identity}\not\Rightarrow\text{copyable}.
\]

And therefore:

\[
\text{perfect-copy theorem}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{universal failure of all non-identity vocabularies}.
\]

This is the precise point at which the original Track 1 candidate fails.

---

## 7. The ambient-Z result survives, but its scope is clearer

The earlier Track 1 question asked whether an ambient Exceptional Zero could distinguish one of two locally symmetric histories.

The answer remains:

> asymmetry elsewhere does not automatically distinguish two candidates here.

However, UCL shows the relevant distinction can come from a **specific external relation** tying one history to an external origin. That is different from merely having some unrelated asymmetry elsewhere.

So the refined principle is:

> Ambient asymmetry is not sufficient; an independently specified relation connecting the candidate to the asymmetry can be sufficient to break the local symmetry.

That relation must still survive the anti-circularity test.

---

## 8. The universal theorem now has a sharper possible form

Track 1 should not ask:

> Do all third-person accounts fail?

That has been broken by UCL.

The more promising question is:

> Can we formally characterize a class of **internally relational** or otherwise restricted vocabularies for which every relevant account admits a perfect relational counterfeit?

This is a narrower theorem-shaped problem because the domain of quantification can potentially be specified.

The distinction between internal and externally anchored descriptions is now important:

\[
\text{internal relational signature}
\]

versus

\[
\text{signature extended by external provenance relations}.
\]

The counterfeit construction is powerful in the first setting. It is not automatically universal in the second.

---

## 9. Status after the survivor

**Original universal candidate: Refuted as stated.**

**Perfect-relational-counterfeit theorem: Derived and intact.**

**Narrower generalization to internally relational signatures: Open.**

The broader recurring-signature investigation remains **Open** because the existence of one survivor does not establish that every proposed third-person account succeeds, only that the exceptionless negative claim fails.

---

## 10. Recommended next step

Do not spend further effort trying to rescue the original universal claim by redefining “third-person” after the fact.

Instead:

1. Define a principled class of internally relational signatures.
2. Prove the counterfeit/non-isolation result for that class if possible.
3. Test whether the class captures the approaches that actually failed in for-this and truth, or whether those domains require different formalisms.
4. In parallel, pressure-test UCL's origin condition rather than assuming the survivor is permanently secure.

The investigation has therefore produced a useful split:

\[
\boxed{\text{copy theorem: secure}}
\]

\[
\boxed{\text{universalization: broken}}
\]

\[
\boxed{\text{restricted structural theorem: open}}
\]
