# State Transition and Causal Sufficiency Test

**Date:** 2026-09-05  
**Status:** Open  
**Type:** Bounded metaphysical / formal pressure test  
**Relation:** Follows the totality/unity investigation and the transition/atemporality line. Tests whether a state transition requires an ontologically external participant.

## 1. Question

Let $O_n$ be a state and $O_{n+1}$ its successor. Does the transition

$$
O_n \rightarrow O_{n+1}
$$

require something outside $O_n$ to participate in producing $O_{n+1}$?

The motivating intuition was specifically **not** that $O_n$ interacts with $O_{n+1}$. Rather, it was that $O_n$ may require some other participant or operative condition in order to transition to $O_{n+1}$.

The test therefore asks whether transition itself entails causal openness.

## 2. Candidate claim

Candidate:

> A state cannot transition merely by being the state it is; something beyond the bare state must participate in making the successor obtain.

A stronger candidate is:

$$
O_n \rightarrow O_{n+1}
\Rightarrow
\exists X\,(X\neq O_n \land X\text{ is external to }O_n\land X\text{ participates in the transition}).
$$

The investigation explicitly tests this stronger inference rather than assuming it.

## 3. Distinctions required

The following must not be conflated:

1. **State:** the condition represented by $O_n$.
2. **Successor:** the condition represented by $O_{n+1}$.
3. **Transition:** the relation or operation connecting them.
4. **Transition specification:** whatever rule, relation, law, disposition, input, or operation determines the successor.
5. **Causal closure:** whether all causally relevant determinants are internal to the chosen system.
6. **Externality:** whether a determinant lies outside the chosen system or state.
7. **Actual exercise:** whether a formally specified transition is actually occurring rather than merely represented.

In particular:

$$
\text{state}\neq\text{transition specification}
$$

and

$$
\text{outside the state}\neq\text{outside the Whole}.
$$

The test also preserves the existing distinctions:

$$
\text{formal succession}\neq\text{temporal succession}
$$

and

$$
\text{formal transition}\neq\text{actual occurrence}.
$$

## 4. Falsification criteria

The candidate is weakened or defeated if any of the following can be established:

- an autonomous formal system has successors determined without an external participant;
- the required transition structure can be included in a larger system containing $O_n$ without being external to that system;
- “outside” turns out to mean only “not explicitly contained in the state description” rather than ontological externality;
- a purported external requirement can be replaced by an internal rule or disposition without changing the transition behavior.

The candidate is strengthened only if an independent argument shows that transition requires an ontologically external participant even when all transition-relevant structure is included in the system.

## 5. Formal pressure test

### 5.1 Autonomous transition

Consider a deterministic transition system with:

$$
O_{n+1}=F(O_n).
$$

No external input is required by the formal transition rule. This provides a direct counterexample to the unrestricted inference:

$$
\text{transition}\Rightarrow\text{external participant}.
$$

**Result:** the strong externality claim fails as a formal necessity.

### 5.2 The transition-specification residue

Although $O_n$ alone does not specify $F$, the pair

$$
(O_n,F)
$$

can determine the successor:

$$
(O_n,F)\rightarrow(O_{n+1},F).
$$

Thus a bare state may be insufficient to explain its successor while a larger closed system remains sufficient.

This establishes:

$$
\boxed{\text{state alone}\neq\text{complete transition explanation}}.
$$

It does **not** establish:

$$
\boxed{\text{transition}\Rightarrow\text{ontological outside}}.
$$

### 5.3 State-relative versus system-relative externality

Suppose $F$ is not a constituent of the state $O_n$ but is a constituent of a larger system:

$$
W_n=(O_n,F).
$$

Then $F$ is external to the state description while internal to $W_n$.

Therefore:

$$
F\notin O_n
$$

does not entail:

$$
F\notin W_n.
$$

This is the decisive scope distinction for the original intuition.

### 5.4 Whole-relative version

Now let $W_n$ be intended as a genuine totality. If transition requires an entity $X$ that is external to $W_n$, then the conjunction

$$
\mathrm{Whole}(W_n)
\land
\mathrm{ExternalCause}(X,W_n)
$$

is incompatible by definition.

But this does not show that a Whole cannot transition. It shows only that **an externally caused transition is incompatible with causal totality**.

The question therefore becomes conditional:

> If transition is shown independently to require an ontologically external participant, then a causally closed Whole cannot transition in that respect.

The antecedent remains unearned.

### 5.5 Actual exercise is not supplied

A formal transition rule $F$ specifies what successor follows under the model. It does not by itself establish that the transition is actually exercised.

Thus:

$$
F(O_n)=O_{n+1}
$$

does not entail:

$$
O_n\text{ actually becomes }O_{n+1}.
$$

This preserves the existing occurrence/performance residue rather than resolving it here.

## 6. Test results

### Result A — Transition does not formally require an external participant

Autonomous transition systems provide counterexamples to the strong claim that every transition requires something external to the transitioning state.

**Status:** Earned as a formal counterexample to the unrestricted externality inference.

### Result B — The bare state is not the complete transition specification

A state considered without its transition law does not, merely by being that state, specify its successor. Additional transition structure may be required.

**Status:** Earned as a conceptual/formal distinction.

### Result C — Additional transition structure need not be outside the larger system

A transition rule can be internal to a larger system containing the state. Therefore:

$$
\text{beyond the bare state}\neq\text{beyond the system}.
$$

**Status:** Earned.

### Result D — Whole-relative externality remains conditional

If a Whole is genuinely exhaustive, then a genuinely external causal participant cannot exist relative to it. But the investigation has not shown that transition requires such a participant.

**Status:** Open conditional pressure point.

### Result E — Transition and actual occurrence remain distinct

The existence of a formal transition specification does not establish actual exercise or occurrence.

**Status:** Earned by continuity with the existing occurrence/performance investigation; no new claim about actuality is promoted here.

## 7. What has not been established

The investigation does **not** establish:

- that transition requires an ontological outside;
- that every state requires an external cause;
- that a causally closed system cannot transition;
- that a Whole cannot transition;
- that transition is temporal;
- that transition is actual occurrence;
- that the transition rule is a distinct ontological entity;
- that the transition rule is primitive;
- that relation or operation is ontologically prior to the states it connects;
- that the Cut has a dynamical consequence.

## 8. Relation to the occurrence/performance investigation

The present test sits one level below the unresolved performance question.

The occurrence investigation already distinguishes formal succession from actual happening and finds that a formal transition structure does not by itself supply actual performance.

The present result adds a separate distinction:

$$
\text{transition specification}\neq\text{external cause}.
$$

The combined picture is:

$$
\text{state}
\neq
\text{transition specification}
\neq
\text{actual exercise}.
$$

No inference from the first distinction to an ontological Beyond is licensed.

## 9. Next pressure

The stronger downstream question is now:

> **What is the ontological status of the transition specification $F$?**

Candidate interpretations include:

- law;
- disposition;
- relation;
- operation;
- input structure;
- primitive transition fact;
- actual exercise.

These should be tested separately. In particular, a new investigation should not simply rename $F$ as “operation” or “act” and treat the new name as an explanation.

## 10. Epistemic status

**Open.**

The investigation defeats the unrestricted inference from transition to ontological externality, while preserving the narrower result that a bare state is not itself a complete transition specification. The surviving unresolved issue is the status of whatever additional structure makes a successor transition formally possible, and separately whether such a specification can account for actual exercise.

The governing rule remains:

> Nothing is stronger than its status.
