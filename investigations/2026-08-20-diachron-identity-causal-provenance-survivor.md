## 1. Question

Does an independently specified causal-provenance relation — specifically, membership in a unique, externally anchored, non-branching causal lineage — survive the Ship-of-Theseus, duplication, fission, convergence, discontinuity, and perfect-counterfeit tests without smuggling diachronic identity into the criterion?

A survivor would not prove that causal lineage **is** personal identity. It would establish only that the recurring-signature claim is not exceptionless over all third-person relational accounts currently under consideration.

---

## 2. Candidate F — Unique Causal Lineage (UCL)

The candidate must be stated without using “same self,” “genuine past,” “mine,” or equivalent target-bearing language.

Let a temporal structure contain events or stages together with an independently specified causal relation \(C\). Let an **origin event** be independently identifiable by a non-identity predicate \(O\) (for example, a specified creation/birth event type, not “the event at which this self begins”).

Define a **lineage segment** as a maximal causal path beginning at a unique origin and continuing through stages for which the causal relation does not branch into two distinct successor paths within the relevant domain. Two stages are UCL-related exactly when they lie on the same such maximal non-branching causal lineage segment.

The candidate therefore says, in substance:

> Diachronic identity is grounded in belonging to the same uniquely originated, non-branching causal lineage.

This is a substantive reduction. It does not merely rename identity as “the relation that preserves identity.”

---

## 3. Anti-circularity check

The defining vocabulary contains:

- temporal structure;
- causal relation;
- independently specified origin condition;
- branching/non-branching structure.

It does **not** contain:

- same-self;
- actual past;
- ownership;
- first-person privilege;
- “genuine” history;
- any predicate defined by the target itself.

Therefore the candidate is independently specifiable before the identity question is asked.

This is important: “unique causal origin” is allowed to be a candidate criterion only when “origin” is independently individuated. If “origin” means “the origin of the self we are trying to identify,” the candidate collapses directly into Construction B and is disqualified.

---

## 4. Test 1 — Ship of Theseus / gradual replacement

### Case

A ship begins in one construction event. Its components are gradually replaced, one at a time, while the ship remains part of one uninterrupted causal process.

### Result

The lineage remains single and non-branching. UCL therefore preserves identity across gradual replacement.

This gives the candidate the persistence result it was designed to obtain without requiring material sameness.

### Pressure

The result depends on causal continuity, not on preservation of parts. A second ship assembled later from the original removed parts has a different causal lineage and therefore is not UCL-identical to the first.

That is a substantive consequence, not a hidden appeal to identity.

**Verdict: survives.**

---

## 5. Test 2 — Perfect duplication

### Case

A stage \(A\) produces two later stages \(B\) and \(C\) with identical internal physical and informational states.

### Naive provenance version

If identity meant merely “shares causal ancestry,” both \(B\) and \(C\) would inherit identity from \(A\). That is incompatible with ordinary numerical identity because it would make one thing identical to two distinct things.

### UCL version

The candidate requires a **non-branching** lineage. At the branching point, the pre-branch lineage ends and two successor lineages begin.

Therefore neither branch is UCL-identical to the predecessor across the split.

This is a substantive philosophical commitment: fission terminates diachronic identity rather than preserving it into both branches.

It is not circular. The criterion was fixed before the test and the result follows from the criterion's graph structure.

**Verdict: survives, with a strong consequence.**

---

## 6. Test 3 — Fission

Fission is the duplication test viewed explicitly as an identity problem.

A, then B and C:

\[
A\to\{B,C\}.
\]

UCL says:

\[
A\not\equiv B,
\qquad
A\not\equiv C
\]

across the branch, because no single non-branching lineage contains A and either successor.

This avoids the contradiction that arises if both successors are declared identical with A.

The cost is that identity can cease at a branching event even though both successors are maximally continuous with the predecessor.

That cost is real, but it is not a failure of the criterion. The test asks whether the account is coherent and independently specified, not whether it agrees with every intuition about fission.

**Verdict: survives.**

---

## 7. Test 4 — Convergence / fusion

### Case

Two distinct causal lineages converge into one later state:

\[
A\to C,
\qquad
B\to C.
\]

UCL cannot place \(C\) in the same non-branching lineage as both A and B. Thus convergence does not preserve identity from both predecessors into one successor.

Depending on the ontology, C is either a newly individuated state/object or a state at which prior identities terminate.

Again, this is coherent with numerical identity: one later thing cannot simply be identical to two numerically distinct earlier things.

**Verdict: survives.**

---

## 8. Test 5 — Temporal discontinuity

A causal lineage can contain a large temporal gap without branching.

UCL does not require uninterrupted observation or uninterrupted phenomenal continuity. It requires only a continuing causal path under the specified relation.

Therefore sleep, unconscious gaps, archival gaps, or other periods without accessible memory do not by themselves destroy identity.

The criterion is temporal-history based, not memory based.

**Verdict: survives.**

---

## 9. Test 6 — Memory loss

Because memory is not part of the criterion, complete memory loss does not alter UCL identity so long as the causal lineage persists without branching.

This is a useful separation from the earlier memory candidate:

\[
\text{memory continuity}\neq\text{causal lineage continuity}.
\]

The implanted-memory case therefore does not defeat UCL in the way it defeats a memory criterion. An implanted memory can match informational content without matching causal provenance.

**Verdict: survives.**

---

## 10. Test 7 — Perfect counterfeit / implanted memory

### Counterfeit construction

Construct a rival stage \(R\) that is internally and informationally indistinguishable from genuine stage \(P\), but whose causal origin and external provenance differ.

Under the earlier relational vocabulary:

\[
P\sim R.
\]

Under UCL, that equivalence is broken if the causal graph includes the independently specified external provenance relation.

If:

\[
O_P\neq O_R
\]

in the relevant causal structure, then:

\[
P\not\equiv_{UCL}R.
\]

No identity predicate has been inserted. The asymmetry comes from a third-person causal fact about their origins.

### Stronger adversarial version

Make the counterfeit internally perfect and copy every relation available to the original model except the actual-world causal provenance linking P to its origin.

UCL still distinguishes them.

This is the first genuine survivor of the current search that defeats the prior perfect-relational-counterfeit construction **by adding an independently specified external relation rather than by adding identity itself**.

### Critical qualification

If the entire universe is expanded so that the counterfeit is also given an isomorphic causal environment and an isomorphic origin structure, then the automorphism problem returns. UCL does not defeat arbitrary symmetry. It defeats the narrower claim that internal temporal/causal/memory structure is the whole relevant structure.

**Verdict: survives the tested counterfeit, while exposing the domain-of-S problem.**

---

## 11. Test 8 — Reconstruction from the original parts

Suppose the original parts of Theseus's ship are later assembled into a second vessel.

The original continuously maintained vessel and the later reconstructed vessel have different causal lineages. UCL therefore assigns identity to the continuous descendant and not to the reconstruction.

This produces a definite answer where purely material criteria can produce conflict.

Again, this is a consequence of the independently stated lineage rule.

**Verdict: survives.**

---

## 12. The decisive pressure: is “unique origin” merely identity in disguise?

This is the central anti-circularity test.

It fails if the candidate says:

> the genuine origin is the origin belonging to the genuine self.

That simply presupposes the target.

It survives if origin is independently specified by structural facts such as a defined creation/birth event, a physical causal boundary, or another non-identity condition that can be recognized without first knowing which later stages are the same self.

The candidate therefore has a genuine dependency:

\[
\text{independently specified origin + causal graph}
\Rightarrow
\text{lineage structure}
\Rightarrow
\text{candidate identity relation}.
\]

Nothing in that chain says “the real self” before the result is obtained.

**Verdict: anti-circularity test passed conditionally on independently specifying the origin predicate.**

---

## 13. New generalization round — what can “origin” mean without carrying identity?

The first survivor result only required a locally independent origin predicate. The next question is whether a general origin rule can be specified without selecting it by the identity verdict it is meant to explain.

### 13.1 Candidate A — earliest event of a specified process type

Define an origin of type \(T\) as the earliest event satisfying an independently specified predicate \(T\), with no earlier causally related event of type \(T\).

This is formally clean once \(T\) is fixed. The problem is that the choice of \(T\) does substantial work.

- Organisms can use biological developmental event types.
- Artifacts can use construction event types.
- Physical processes can use a specified onset condition.
- Persons may admit several plausible event types.

The remaining question is:

> What independently privileges \(T\) as the identity-relevant kind of event?

Answering “the kind that gives the desired identity verdict” is disallowed.

**Verdict: locally viable; domain-general relevance remains open.**

### 13.2 Candidate B — minimal causal root

Define the origin as the minimal causal root from which the later trajectory is generated.

This handles simple production chains but encounters multiple causal parents, overdetermination, and shared origins. A principled ordering over causes is required to identify the minimal root.

If that ordering is tuned to identity cases, the candidate becomes target-bearing.

**Verdict: underdefined without an independent causal-minimality principle.**

### 13.3 Candidate C — unique causal ancestor under a complete causal graph

Require a unique ancestor satisfying an independently specified causal characterization.

This works when uniqueness actually exists, but the criterion does not guarantee uniqueness. Many entities have multiple causes, shared production events, or mergers.

Declaring identity to exist only when unique origin exists is coherent but substantively revisionary. Adding rules for multiple origins reopens the selection problem.

**Verdict: coherent restricted theory; not a general identity criterion yet.**

### 13.4 Candidate D — counterfactual causal indispensability

Define the relevant origin as an event whose absence, under a specified intervention model, would prevent the later stage from existing in the relevant form.

This does not by itself select a unique cause under causal overdetermination, and the result can depend on which interventions and background variables are held fixed.

Fission does not help: one predecessor event can be counterfactually indispensable to multiple successors.

**Verdict: genuine causal relation; insufficient as an identity criterion.**

### 13.5 Candidate E — maximal non-branching causal lineage without origin

Dropping origin entirely removes the origin-selection problem but also removes the external asymmetry that defeated the perfect internal counterfeit. A counterfeit can possess its own maximal non-branching lineage.

Branch and merge points also require a principled segmentation rule if the resulting relation is to be an equivalence relation over stages.

**Verdict: fails to preserve the decisive feature of UCL.**

## 14. Equivalence-relation pressure

A genuine theory should yield a coherent diachronic identity relation.

If UCL-relatedness is membership in a unique-origin, non-branching lineage segment, reflexivity and symmetry are straightforward once the segments are fixed. Transitivity follows if the segments form a genuine partition of stages.

The unresolved issue is how branch and merge events are assigned to segments. A stage at a branch point could belong to a predecessor segment, to successor segments, or to a newly created segment. The choice must be fixed independently rather than made to recover an intended identity verdict.

This is now a formal subproblem of the theory, not merely an intuitive objection.

---

## 15. What has actually survived

The survivor is **not** causal continuity alone. It is the stronger relation:

\[
\boxed{\text{independently specified origin + causal provenance + non-branching continuation}}.
\]

It earns its asymmetry from external causal structure rather than internal resemblance, memory, or informational content.

That remains enough to defeat the original exceptionless claim that every relevant third-person account must admit a perfect internal counterfeit or insert identity by hand.

But the general theory remains under pressure because the origin-selection principle has not yet been generalized non-arbitrarily.

---

## 16. Status

### Earned

- Local independent origin predicates exist.
- UCL breaks the perfect internal-counterfeit construction when the counterfeit differs in external causal provenance.
- UCL survives the current Ship-of-Theseus, duplication, fission, convergence, discontinuity, memory-loss, and reconstruction tests under its explicit assumptions.
- Several natural generalizations of “origin” have now been pressure-tested and none has yet supplied a domain-general criterion without a new selection problem.

### Open

- A domain-general, non-arbitrary origin-selection rule.
- Whether the resulting lineage relation is a principled equivalence relation on general causal structures.
- Whether the fission/merger termination behavior is an acceptable identity result or merely a consequence of the chosen persistence criterion.
- Whether “causal provenance” explains identity or merely supplies a distinct historical relation that happens to track identity in the tested cases.

### Not earned

- That UCL is the correct theory of personal identity.
- That every person/entity has one uniquely relevant origin.
- That the Exceptional Zero explains UCL or diachronic identity.
- That the recurring signature is restored by abandoning internal relations.

---

## 17. Recommended next test

Do not generate more origin rules immediately.

The next move should be to **formalize the selection problem itself**.

Define a candidate class of origin predicates \(\mathcal O\) and ask:

> What properties must an origin predicate satisfy to be independent of identity, stable under isomorphic redescription, and sufficient to generate a non-branching lineage relation?

Then test the candidate axioms against:

- multiple causal parents;
- overdetermination;
- shared production events;
- cloning;
- twinning;
- fusion;
- reconstruction;
- branching processes;
- temporally separated continuation.

If no plausible axiomatization survives, UCL may settle into a **Boundary for reduction to causal provenance** rather than merely remaining an open candidate.

For now, however, that stronger status is not earned.
