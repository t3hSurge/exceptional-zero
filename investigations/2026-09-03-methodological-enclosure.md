## 12. Do not name the missing slot “methodological inadequacy”

The missing recognition-type may be more primitive than “$A$ is inadequate.”

A better first name is **model-space failure**:

> The model space itself has failed to contain the kind of object required by the question.

$A$ recognizes failures of objects *inside* a model space. It recognizes that a model space can be incompletely searched. It does not yet have a specified event for: the space does not contain the required kind.

This is the next distinction after a distinction the project already uses.

Already earned in other files:

$$
\text{candidate failure}\neq\text{class failure}.
$$

Proposed, not assumed:

$$
\text{class failure}\neq\text{vocabulary failure}.
$$

$$
\text{vocabulary failure}\neq\text{methodological failure}.
$$

Hierarchy under test:

$$
\text{candidate}\ \subset\ \text{candidate class}\ \subset\ \text{admissible vocabulary}\ \subset\ \text{methodology}.
$$

The project has mechanisms for interrogating the lower levels. The open question is whether it has a mechanism for moving up a level when lower-level failures accumulate.

“Model-space failure” and the two new $\neq$ claims are **hypotheses about the type theory of failure**. They are not results. They are not Meta-Boundary.
