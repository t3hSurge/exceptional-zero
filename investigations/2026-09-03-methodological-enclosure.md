# Methodological Enclosure

**Date:** 2026-09-03  
**Status:** Open third-order investigation  
**Depends on:** the project's existing status architecture and the AT / verb-first enclosure-shaped results  
**Not in scope:** canonical status-table revision; movers; time primitives; `G*_2`; `L`; `\Sigma`; escape hatches (external intuition, phenomenology, revelation)

---

## 1. Why this is third-order

The first-order work tests claims. The second-order work tests candidate classes for those claims (candidate failure $\neq$ class failure). This investigation tests the **admissibility architecture** that decides what may count as a candidate, a counterexample, or evidence at all.

The two upstream investigations have already produced the same shape at object-level:

- “atemporal” cannot yet be specified without risking import of the temporal/performance content it is meant to exclude;
- “verb-first” cannot yet be specified without risking import of occurrence/performance.

The present question is whether the methodology can generate a failure of its own standards without presupposing those standards in the test.

$$
\boxed{\text{Can a methodology establish a non-circular criterion by which its own admissibility conditions could be shown inadequate?}}
$$

No new status category is adopted here. “Meta-Boundary” is recorded only as a *candidate label* for a possible later result.

---

## 2. Suspected failure mode

**Methodological Enclosure:** the methodology can detect failures of claims inside its admissible epistemic space, while remaining unable to detect that its own admissibility conditions systematically exclude the relevant truth.

Let $A$ be the current admissibility architecture (status vocabulary, candidate-class rules, anti-circularity, target-shift, Actualization Boundary, candidate/class distinction).

The enclosure hypothesis is that every observation that could count against $A$ must first be translated into $A$'s vocabulary. Then:

$$
A\ \text{determines what counts as evidence against }A.
$$

That is the circularity to be tested, not assumed.

## 3. Diagnostic battery

### M1. Internal error

Can the methodology detect “I applied my own rules incorrectly”?

**Yes.** Ordinary correction of misapplied inference, scope, or status. Not the target.

### M2. Internal overreach

Can it detect “the rules were applied correctly, but the result was promoted too far”?

**Yes.** The repository already does this routinely (candidate $\neq$ class; Open $\neq$ Boundary; pointer $\neq$ theory). Not the target.

### M3. Missing candidate

Can it detect “the answer exists, but the right candidate has not been found”?

**Yes, in form.** That is what Open and the candidate/class distinction are for. Still not the target.

### M4. Missing category

Can it detect that the thing sought does not belong to any category it currently permits as a candidate?

This is the live cut.

Suppose some $X$ is real, but $X$ is not a member of the present candidate class. Then every proposed $X_i$ fails by import, circularity, static collapse, or other admissibility violation. From inside, “not found yet,” “inexpressible here,” and “the vocabulary is the obstruction” are not yet distinguished.

$$
\boxed{\text{M1--M3 are already handled.}\qquad\text{M4 is the enclosure test.}}
$$

## 14. Stall test — AT criterion

This is a stall test, not an escalation test. The stall is the already-recorded AT result: no tested non-circular criterion for “atemporal” has been found, and a universal negative is not earned.

### Step 1. The stall is genuine

The AT file stops with an Open about the admissibility of “atemporal” itself. That is not a misapplied rule (M1) and not an over-promoted Boundary (M2).

### Step 2. What event-types does $A$ assign?

The stall is already classified, in combination, as:

- candidate failure (each AT criterion failed);
- class underdetermination (the class of criteria is not exhausted);
- circularity (some criteria import temporal content);
- representation failure (the word “atemporal” does not independently pick a model class);
- deeper-question isolation (the domain of admissible atemporal facts is itself un-bounded).

So $A$ *does* assign event-types. The stall is not silent.

### Step 3. Do those assignments capture the phenomenon?

Only partly. Each assigned type treats the model space as given and reports a failure *inside* it, or reports that search of that space is incomplete. None of them is the claim:

> the space in which “atemporal criterion” is being sought may not contain objects of the required kind.

Class underdetermination says: more criteria might work. Model-space failure would say: criteria of this kind may be the wrong kind. $A$ currently has the first sentence. It does not have a specified recognition rule for the second.

### Step 4. Record the inventory gap

$$
\boxed{
\text{AT stall: assigned as mixed lower-level events; model-space failure names the unassigned remainder.}
}
$$

“Model-space failure” here is a description of the gap in the inventory. It is not an event $A$ has detected. It is not a verdict that the AT class is empty. It is not a verdict that $A$ is inadequate.

### Step 5. Does $A$ have a rule that recognizes that gap as a gap?

No specified rule. Deeper-question isolation is the nearest existing type: it licenses moving the question upstream. It does not license concluding that the *level* of representation has failed. Isolation of a deeper question still assumes the new question is askable in the same event ontology.

### Step 6. Higher-order recognition is not shown to have failed

Because the recognition rule has not been constructed, its failure cannot be observed. Accumulation of AT candidate failures therefore does **not** license ascent to methodological inadequacy.

## 17. Representational self-audit — three preserved outcomes

$$
\boxed{
\text{Can }A\text{ detect failure of the representational level on which }A\text{ itself operates?}
}
$$

The detecting level cannot be $A$ wearing a different hat.

Three outcomes worth preserving. None is selected.

1. **Independent detector exists.** Then model-space failure can actually be tested.
2. **Every proposed detector presupposes the representational capacity under examination.** Then there is a genuine self-audit stall.
3. **Something else emerges.** Record it without forcing it into (1) or (2).

Even outcome 2 does not establish:

$$
\text{self-audit bottoms out}\;\Rightarrow\; A\text{ is inadequate}.
$$

It would establish only that the present investigation cannot discriminate “boundary” from “unfinished method.” That is a methodological result precisely because it refuses to become a metaphysical result.

$$

is the correct result about the kind of boundary. It is not a filling of the $?$.
