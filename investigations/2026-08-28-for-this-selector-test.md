# For-This as a Candidate Identity Selector

**Date:** 2026-08-28  
**Status:** Complete for the present candidate  
**Canonical status affected:** None

## 1. Question

Can phenomenal / first-person continuity of the framework's **for-this** supply a determinate causal-relation selector `C_*` for diachronic identity?

The candidate is deliberately narrower than informational continuity. It asks whether there is a phenomenal continuity relation between the for-this at two stages that can privilege one persistence path over another.

No identity predicate is assumed in the candidate definition. No `Σ` is introduced or promoted.

## 2. Specification test

A candidate can be stated schematically as

`F(S_0,S_1) := for-this(S_0) ∧ for-this(S_1) ∧ R(S_0,S_1)`.

The specification of `for-this` itself is inherited from Part Four as an established primitive. The pressure is therefore on the relational component `R`.

## 3. Circularity test

The obvious formulation is:

> the for-this at `S_1` is experienced as belonging to the same subject as the for-this at `S_0`.

This fails immediately. “Same subject” is target-bearing language and simply reinstates the identity relation the candidate is supposed to explain.

**Verdict:** circular formulation rejected.

## 4. Target-shift test

A weaker proposal says that the later for-this contains apparent memory content of the earlier for-this.

That is informational / memory continuity. It falls directly under the previously tested perfect-counterfeit construction: an implanted-memory counterfeit can reproduce the same apparent content without inheriting the proposed identity relation.

**Verdict:** target shift to an already-tested informational criterion.

## 5. Phenomenal remainder test

Remove memory content and retain only alleged phenomenal flow:

> there is something it is like for `S_1` that phenomenally flows from what it was like for `S_0`.

This formulation avoids explicit identity vocabulary, but the relation “flows from” is not independently third-person checkable under the Part Four for-this boundary. No available physical or informational signature distinguishes genuine phenomenal flow from a qualitatively identical fresh phenomenal instantiation.

Thus the candidate is underdetermined rather than circular at this stage.

**Verdict:** no determinate third-person selector is supplied.

## 6. Theseus pressure

The phenomenal-flow relation does not attach cleanly to either process continuity or material provenance. Both can be held fixed while the phenomenal relation remains unobservable from outside.

It therefore cannot arbitrate the `P/M` disagreement in `C_*`.

**Verdict:** category mismatch with the causal-selection problem; no positive selector.

## 7. Fission pressure

If two successors both satisfy the stipulated phenomenal-flow relation to the predecessor, the candidate supplies no identity-neutral way to choose one.

If only one is declared to have genuine flow, that declaration is not independently grounded by the candidate.

**Verdict:** no determinate tie-breaker.

## 8. Counterfeit pressure

An informational counterfeit can, in principle, be stipulated to have a phenomenal state qualitatively indistinguishable from the genuine continuation. The framework's existing for-this result provides no third-person criterion that excludes such a case.

If the candidate simply stipulates that the genuine process has “real” phenomenal flow while the counterfeit lacks it, that stipulation performs the identity work by hand.

**Verdict:** counterfeit distinction is not independently earned.

## 9. Representation pressure

Across the attempted formulations, the candidate follows one of two paths:

- identity-bearing language re-enters (`same subject`, genuine flow, etc.); or
- the relation becomes phenomenal and therefore underdetermined from the third-person standpoint; or
- memory/content is reintroduced, collapsing into informational continuity.

No version simultaneously remains non-circular, non-shifted, third-person discriminable, and determinate.

## 10. Result

The test earns the following narrow result:

`for-this` does not supply a determinate `C_*` under the tested formulations.

The failure modes are specifically:

1. **Circularity** when phenomenal continuity is defined through sameness of subject.
2. **Target-shift** when the candidate is reduced to memory/informational continuity.
3. **Underdetermination** when phenomenal flow is retained without informational content.

This is a cross-check against the Part Four for-this boundary, not a claim that phenomenal experience is unreal or dispensable.

## 11. Status

**For-this as a determinate `C_*`: failed candidate.**  
**For-this itself: no status change.**  
**Diachronic identity reduction: remains Open / incomplete.**  
**`Σ`: untouched.**

The result does not close the broader search for identity selectors. Candidate failure is not class failure.
