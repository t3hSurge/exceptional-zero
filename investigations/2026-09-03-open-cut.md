## OC3 — Does indefinite revisability produce a genuine contradiction?

Target is a *logical* inconsistency. Not inconvenience, labor, discomfort, or failure to reach a last word.

Not under test: “every claim is actively reconsidered at every moment.” That is a stronger premise and would manufacture a contradiction.

Formulation under test:

$$
X\ \text{is provisionally accepted at }t
\quad\text{and}\quad
X\ \text{remains revisable.}
$$

### Claims

Does $\mathrm{Accepted}_t(X)$ contradict revisability of $X$?

No. Acceptance-at-$t$ is a historical-operational fact. Revisability is a modal fact about later inquiry. They take different objects.

$$
\mathrm{Accepted}_t(X)\;\not\Rightarrow\; X\text{ is permanently true.}
$$

Reopening later does not falsify “$X$ was accepted at $t$.” It can change whether $X$ is still accepted.

### Rules

Let $R^\ast$ be the sentence “all rules remain revisable,” used only as a test object, not as an adopted axiom.

If $R^\ast$ is itself revisable, is that a contradiction?

Only if $R^\ast$ is read as *unrevisably* asserting its own unrevisable hold. That reading installs closure inside the rule.

Read as: $R^\ast$ is presently held, and a later inquiry may withdraw it. Then holding $R^\ast$ at $t$ and leaving $R^\ast$ open to withdrawal is the same pattern as $\mathrm{Accepted}_t(X)$ above. No inconsistency isolated.

If later inquiry *withdraws* $R^\ast$, that is not $R^\ast$ and $\neg R^\ast$ at the same time. It is $R^\ast$ at $t$ and not-$R^\ast$ at $t'$.

A contradiction would require holding both at one time, or a sentence that cannot be stated without denying what it states. The tested reading does not do that.

(The liar-adjacent reading “this sentence is unrevisably revisable” is a different object. It was not adopted. Manufacturing that reading to kill $R^\ast$ is not a result against the weaker pair above.)

### Stopping points

Can “this investigation is complete at this level” remain revisable without making “complete” meaningless?

Yes, if complete means: no warranted next move is *presently* identified for *this* defined task.

That content is compatible with later identification of a new warranted move. The earlier status remains a true report of the earlier evidential situation.

“Complete” becomes meaningless only if it is defined as “no future move is possible.” That is the OC1 stipulation again.

### Revision

Does reopening require *negating* the earlier provisional status?

No. The earlier status can remain historically true while the substantive claim changes.

$$
\mathrm{Accepted}_t(X)\ \land\ \neg\mathrm{Accepted}_{t'}(X)
$$

is not a contradiction.

### What was not counted as contradiction

Endless labor. No last word. Discomfort at the enclosure stall. The possibility that some later inquiry withdraws a present rule.
