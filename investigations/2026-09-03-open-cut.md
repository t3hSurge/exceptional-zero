---

## 1. Core question

$$
\boxed{\text{Does unrestricted revisability require a terminal boundary at all?}}
$$

Sharper form:

Can a methodology remain open to reassessing its claims, rules, criteria, and stopping points without collapsing into contradiction or paralysis?

Prior question to the enclosure test:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Was there ever a warrant for treating “not knowing where to continue” as something that needs resolution?}
}
$$

---

## 2. Candidate rule, not axiom

$$
R := \text{every claim, criterion, rule, and stopping point remains in principle revisable.}
$$

$R$ is under test. It is not installed.

“In principle revisable” does not by itself require infinite *active* reassessment.

---

## 3. Three notions, not one

$$
\boxed{\text{termination}} \qquad \boxed{\text{completion}} \qquad \boxed{\text{closure}}
$$

- **Termination:** no warranted next move is presently identified.
- **Completion (at this level):** the defined task has reached its current justified endpoint.
- **Closure:** the result is exempt from future reassessment.

Candidate proposition, not derived:

$$
\mathrm{Complete}(I) \;\not\Rightarrow\; \mathrm{Final}(I)
$$

A notebook can close tonight without a metaphysically mandatory end of inquiry.

---

## 4. Attack order

OC5 is last. Do not begin by concluding it.

### OC1. Is terminal closure necessary?

For any proposed necessity $N$ (final foundation; terminal stopping rule; privileged existence criterion; permanent inside/outside of the method; final admissibility criterion), ask: why $N$?

If the answer introduces $N'$, reassess $N'$.

If the answer says recursion must terminate, demand the warrant.

If the answer says recursion need not terminate, ask whether that is a contradiction or only the abandonment of a demand for global completion.

### OC2. Is provisional stopping sufficient?

Test whether

$$
\text{inquiry must eventually stop}
$$

is required, or whether

$$
\text{a particular inquiry may provisionally stop}
$$

is enough for the methodology to function.

### OC3. Does indefinite revisability create contradiction?

Look for a real contradiction, not inconvenience, not fatigue, not “it never finishes.” Pragmatic facts about an agent are not yet metaphysical facts about inquiry.

### OC4. Can any purported final criterion survive its own reassessment?

If yes, $R$ is constrained. If every such criterion falls by applying $R$ to itself, record that without promoting “no criterion is possible.”

### OC5. Is the demand for terminal closure itself an illicit hidden assumption?

Last. Not the opening conclusion.

---

## 5. Hypothesis to kill

$$
H_{\mathrm{OC}}:\ \text{demanding a terminal boundary illicitly converts provisional inquiry into metaphysical closure.}
$$

Also under attack, not assumed:

$$
\boxed{\text{The demand for a final boundary is itself the Cut.}}
$$

Search for:

- a non-Cut explanation of why methodologies require closure;
- a genuine contradiction from indefinite revisability;
- a case where provisional closure *must* become permanent;
- a principled distinction between “open” and “unfinished” that does not reintroduce the boundary it was meant to justify.

---

## 6. Forbidden promotions

$$
\text{no warrant for closure found} \;\not\Rightarrow\; \text{closure is impossible.}
$$

$$
\text{provisional stopping is coherent} \;\not\Rightarrow\; \text{all inquiry must remain indefinitely active.}
$$

$$
\text{the pattern is beautiful} \;\not\Rightarrow\; \text{the pattern is derived.}
$$

Do not replace the enclosure endpoint with “the enclosure investigation was wrong.”

Relation intended:

$$
\text{previous endpoint} \;\rightarrow\; \text{reassessment of the demand imposed on that endpoint.}
$$
