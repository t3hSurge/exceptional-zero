# Interest-Relative Diachronic Identity: The Ship of Theseus as a Boundary of Reduction

**Status:** Open investigation  
**Scope:** Whether the unresolved selection of `C_*` is not a missing target-independent causal fact, but an interest-relative selection performed by a reaching subject  
**Depends on:** Joint 3 (`I cannot not reach`); UCL battery; representation-invariance investigation; `J_cut` held fixed  
**No new causal family introduced. No `Σ` candidate promoted.**

---

## 1. The Claim Under Investigation

The current UCL investigation reached a precise underdetermination: Ship of Theseus admits at least two independently stateable causal continuities — process continuity and material continuity — that yield opposite positive persistence verdicts. No target-independent rule internal to the current causal architecture selects between them.

The present hypothesis is that this is not an accidental incompleteness of `C_*`.

> **Diachronic identity may be interest-relative causality: physical history supplies multiple continuities, while a reaching subject selects a causal thread for purposes of tracking.**

On this hypothesis, the demand that a purely descriptive causal structure determine the identity-relevant continuation is a category error. The world contains causal continuities; it does not contain a further physical label saying which continuity is *the* identity-bearing one.

This is a new philosophical claim. It is **Open**, not retroactively treated as established by the preceding UCL battery.

---

## 2. What the Ship of Theseus Result Actually Shows

Let the same replacement history contain at least two causal relations:

\[
C_{process}, \qquad C_{material}.
\]

Under a process-continuity reading, Ship A is the continuation and Ship B is a splice. Under a material-continuity reading, Ship B is the continuation and Ship A has lost its original substrate.

Both descriptions can be stated in third-person causal language. Neither requires the words `same ship`, `genuine`, or `identity` in its definition.

The important observation is therefore not merely:

> UCL cannot choose.

It is:

> **There may be nothing in the physical causal description for UCL to choose.**

The failure of `C_*` to select one relation may be the correct description of the causal facts themselves.

The universe contains both continuities. The identity question adds another question: **which continuity is being tracked, and for what purpose?**

---

## 3. The Reaching Subject as the Selection Layer

Joint 3 is currently held as the belief:

> **I cannot not reach.**

The proposed extension is deliberately narrower than the old universal claim `we cannot not reach`. It concerns the subject making the identity attribution.

A finite reaching subject cannot preserve every possible causal relation as equally salient for every act of tracking. To follow a ship, person, artifact, institution, or other diachronic object through time is already to select some thread through a history containing many relations.

The proposed structure is therefore:

\[
\text{causal history}
\rightarrow
\text{reaching subject}
\rightarrow
\text{interest / purpose of tracking}
\rightarrow
\text{selected causal thread}
\rightarrow
\text{identity attribution}.
\]

The selection is not necessarily a conscious decision. A historian, sailor, engineer, conservator, court, or ordinary observer may track different continuities because different practical questions make different relations salient.

This does **not** imply that identity judgments are arbitrary. The causal history constrains the available continuities; the subject's interest determines which of those continuities is being tracked as the relevant one.

---

## 4. Theseus as a Positive Case for Interest-Relative Tracking

The standard fork can be restated without asking which vessel is metaphysically the "real" ship.

### Historian / relic-tracking

If the purpose is preservation of the historical artifact's original material, the material lineage is the relevant thread. Ship B is tracked as the continuation of the original material artifact.

### Sailor / function-tracking

If the purpose is continuity of the operational vessel through repairs, the process/functional lineage is the relevant thread. Ship A is tracked as the continuation of the working ship.

The hypothesis does not require either judgment to be *the* objectively correct answer to a single identity question. They are answers to differently specified tracking questions.

Thus the apparent contradiction may arise from silently changing the tracking purpose while pretending the word "same" has remained fully fixed.

---

## 5. What This Does to the `G*` Search

The representation-invariance investigation asked for a target-independent common structure `G*` from which competing causal descriptions could be recovered and on which a later `Σ` could operate.

The present hypothesis challenges the target-independence requirement at the final selection step, not at the descriptive level.

A neutral causal structure may be expected to preserve both process and material relations:

\[
G^* \supseteq \{C_{process}, C_{material}, \ldots\}.
\]

It then should **not** determine which relation is identity-relevant. That determination belongs to the tracking relation between a subject and a history.

This gives a possible reinterpretation of the failed `G*_1` candidate:

> `G*_1` failed as an identity-solving selective structure because its physical membership boundary could not independently choose the relation that identity should track.

That failure does not show that a neutral causal structure is impossible. It may show that neutrality and identity-selection were being asked of the same object when they belong to different layers.

The distinction is:

\[
\boxed{\text{representation-useful} \neq \text{identity-useful}}
\]

and now potentially:

\[
\boxed{\text{causal-description} \neq \text{identity-attribution}.}
\]

---

## 6. Proposed Reformulation of `C_*`

The earlier architecture was:

\[
O \rightarrow [\Sigma] \rightarrow C_* \rightarrow J_{cut}.
\]

Under the present hypothesis, the missing selection is not a purely third-person selector hidden inside `C_*`.

A more faithful architecture may be:

\[
O \rightarrow C_{available} \rightarrow J_{cut}
\]

followed by:

\[
(\text{subject},\ \text{interest},\ \text{history})
\rightarrow C_{tracked}
\rightarrow \text{identity attribution}.
\]

`C_available` names the causal continuities present in the history without ranking them. `C_tracked` is the continuity selected for a specified act of tracking.

This is **not yet a replacement formalism**. The notation records the hypothesis that the selection operation belongs to a subject-history relation rather than to the physical causal graph alone.

---

## 7. Why This Is Not Merely "Identity Is Subjective"

The hypothesis should not be weakened into the ordinary claim that identity is "whatever someone feels it is."

The proposed position has three constraints:

1. **Causal constraint:** the selected thread must be present in the causal history. A subject cannot manufacture an arbitrary physical ancestry by declaration.
2. **Interest constraint:** different tracking purposes may select different causal threads without one being a physical error.
3. **Attribution constraint:** the identity verdict concerns the relation between a subject and a history, not an additional physical property hidden inside the history.

The resulting objectivity is therefore relational rather than intrinsic:

\[
\boxed{\text{identity attribution} = \text{causal history} + \text{mode of tracking}.}
\]

The causal facts remain objective. What is interest-relative is which causal fact is being used to answer the identity question.

---

## 8. The Stronger Claim and Its Burden

The strongest proposed claim is:

> **No target-independent, representation-invariant descriptive causal structure can by itself determine diachronic identity, because identity attribution requires a purpose of tracking supplied by a reaching subject.**

This is stronger than the established result that the present UCL formulation does not determine `C_*`.

The stronger claim therefore remains **Open**.

To earn it, the investigation must establish more than another failed candidate. It must show why the subject/interest layer is not merely one more optional causal feature that a future target-independent theory could replace.

In particular, the argument must resist the possibility that a future formal structure could encode the relevant tracking relation without simply renaming it as an objective physical feature.

---

## 9. The Joint 3 Pressure

The key proposed bridge is:

\[
\text{I cannot not reach}
\Rightarrow
\text{I cannot track everything equally}
\Rightarrow
\text{I must select a thread for diachronic tracking}.
\]

Only the first statement is currently an established belief in the framework.

The second and third statements do not automatically follow from the first. A finite subject may have limited attention, memory, or practical capacity, but those facts are not yet formal consequences of Joint 3 as currently stated.

This is the first major pressure point.

A successful argument must avoid silently replacing:

> `I cannot not reach`

with the stronger and different claim:

> `I must choose exactly one causal relation as identity-relevant.`

The latter needs its own argument.

## 10. Adversarial Tests

### Test IR-1 — Multiple legitimate interests

Construct a Theseus history and specify two tracking purposes independently: material preservation and operational continuity.

**Pass condition:** both identity attributions are intelligible without treating one as physically false merely because the other was selected.

### Test IR-2 — Interest change without physical change

Hold the complete causal history fixed while changing only the purpose of tracking.

**Question:** Can the identity attribution change without implying that the physical history changed?

If yes, that supports the distinction between causal fact and identity attribution.

### Test IR-3 — Physical change without interest change

Hold the tracking purpose fixed while altering the causal history.

**Question:** Does the available identity attribution change in response to the causal facts?

If no, the theory has become arbitrary. If yes, the causal constraint is preserved.

### Test IR-4 — Arbitrary declaration

Ask whether a subject can simply declare an unrelated object to be the continuation of the original while having no causal thread connecting them.

**Expected pressure:** the interest-relative account must reject arbitrary declaration because the selected thread must be causally available.

### Test IR-5 — Perfect counterfeit

The implanted-memory case should remain a clean exclusion when the relevant tracking purpose is fixed. A counterfeit whose relevant causal provenance does not descend from the original should not become identical merely because an observer wants to track it as such.

This preserves the strongest earned UCL result.

### Test IR-6 — Fission

If one causal history branches into two continuations, the subject may track one branch, both branches, or the pre-branch history depending on the purpose. The account must distinguish this plural tracking from the claim that both branches are unambiguously one numerically identical continuation.

### Test IR-7 — Fusion

If two causal histories converge, the subject may track the resulting object as continuous with either or both antecedents depending on purpose. The theory must not silently convert relational tracking into a unique metaphysical identity fact.

### Test IR-8 — Interest-invariance requirement

Ask whether two subjects with genuinely identical tracking purposes and access to the same causal history must agree in their identity attribution.

If disagreement remains possible, the theory needs an account of whether the disagreement is factual, evidential, or merely terminological.

### Test IR-9 — Hidden target dependence

Check whether the phrase "the causal thread that matters to the subject" is merely `Σ` renamed.

A successful account must specify what counts as an interest/purpose of tracking without defining it as "whatever produces the desired identity verdict."

### Test IR-10 — Universalization pressure

Ask whether the account can be generalized from the present self without returning to the previously rejected universal `we cannot not reach`.

The safe form is local: a given reaching subject necessarily operates under some mode of tracking. Any broader anthropological or metaphysical claim requires separate support.

---

## 11. What Would Count as Success

This investigation would earn a positive result if it establishes all of the following:

1. The Ship of Theseus fork can be represented as divergence between legitimate causal continuities rather than as competition between one true and one false physical history.
2. A specified tracking purpose can select among those continuities without altering the underlying causal facts.
3. The selection is constrained by actual causal availability and therefore is not arbitrary subjectivism.
4. The perfect-counterfeit result remains intact when the tracking purpose is held fixed.
5. The subject/interest layer cannot be eliminated without reintroducing a hidden equivalent of the same selection operation.
6. The bridge from Joint 3 to necessary tracking can be stated without upgrading Joint 3 into an unsupported universal claim.

Only then would the stronger category-error diagnosis be earned.

---

## 12. What Would Count as a Boundary

A Boundary would be earned if the investigation shows that every attempt to derive identity solely from target-independent causal structure either:

- leaves multiple legitimate causal continuities unresolved;
- hides the selection criterion inside the representation or membership rule;
- becomes arbitrary by ignoring causal constraints; or
- effectively reintroduces a subject-relative tracking relation under another name.

Such a Boundary would be stronger than the present UCL Boundary because it would explain *why* the causal-selection search fails rather than merely recording that the current candidates fail.

It would still not prove that no possible formalism can ever represent identity in any sense. It would establish a Boundary specifically against **purely descriptive, target-independent reduction of diachronic identity**.

---

## 13. Current Status

**Open.**

What is already earned:

- Ship of Theseus exposes genuine underdetermination in the present `C_*` architecture.
- Process and material continuity are both independently describable causal relations.
- `G*_1` failed as a concrete selective common structure; this does not close the selective class.
- The perfect-counterfeit / implanted-memory result remains robust.
- The framework already contains the local reaching claim `I cannot not reach` as an explicit belief.

What is **not** yet earned:

- that Joint 3 entails a necessity to select a causal thread;
- that all diachronic identity is interest-relative;
- that no target-independent identity criterion could exist in principle;
- that Ship of Theseus is conclusively a category error rather than an unresolved metaphysical question.

### Locked next move

Do **not** construct `G*_2` merely to continue the candidate sequence.

Do **not** promote interest-relative identity to Boundary.

The next pressure is the bridge itself:

\[
\boxed{\text{I cannot not reach}\;\rightarrow\;\text{necessary tracking selection}.}
\]

The investigation should attack that bridge directly, while preserving the earned causal constraints and the distinction between a subject's tracking purpose and the physical causal history being tracked.

---

## 14. Provisional Canonical Formulation

The strongest formulation currently justified for discussion is:

> **The Ship of Theseus may not be asking for a hidden physical identity fact at all. Physical history contains multiple legitimate continuities. A finite reaching subject tracks one or another of those continuities for some purpose, and the resulting identity attribution belongs to that subject-history relation rather than to the causal history alone.**

This is the hypothesis under investigation, not yet a canonical result.
