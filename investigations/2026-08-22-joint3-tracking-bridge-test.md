# Joint 3 → Necessary Tracking Selection: Bridge Test

**Status:** Open investigation  
**Scope:** Whether Joint 3 (`I cannot not reach`) entails selecting a causal thread for diachronic tracking  
**No `G*_2`. No `Σ`. No process/material preference.**

---

## 1. The bridge

The interest-relative hypothesis proposes:

\[
I\text{ cannot not reach}
\Rightarrow
I\text{ cannot track everything equally}
\Rightarrow
I\text{ must select a causal thread for diachronic tracking}.
\]

Only the first statement is currently Joint 3. The purpose of this investigation is to determine whether the latter statements follow from it or require an additional premise.

The test is hostile to the new hypothesis: failure of the bridge does not refute interest-relative identity, but it prevents the thesis from borrowing Joint 3's status without further argument.

## 2. Joint 3 cannot be silently strengthened

The canonical framework deliberately reduced the earlier universal `we cannot not reach` to the local `I cannot not reach`. It also explicitly refuses the inference that finitude causes reaching: the established formulation is “I am finite, and I reach,” not “I am finite, therefore I reach.” Joint 3 is a belief, not a theorem.

Therefore it cannot silently become:

- `I am finite, therefore I must select one thread`;
- `I reach, therefore I attend to only one relation`;
- `I track, therefore exactly one causal relation is identity-relevant`;
- `identity is whatever I want it to be`.

Each requires separate support.

## 3. Test J3-1 — Logical entailment

Let `R(I)` be `I cannot not reach` and `S(I)` be `I must select a causal thread for tracking`.

A countermodel is possible: a reaching subject can represent several causal relations simultaneously,

\[
T(I,H)=\{C_{process},C_{material},C_{functional},C_{informational},\ldots\},
\]

while refusing to declare any one of them *the* identity-bearing relation. Nothing in `R(I)` itself forbids this.

**Result:**

\[
\boxed{J3\not\Rightarrow\text{unique identity-thread selection}}
\]

This is a direct non-entailment, not a refutation of the interest-relative thesis.

## 4. Test J3-2 — Does “reach” already contain selection?

Reaching plausibly implies directedness, but directedness does not imply the relevant kind of selection. A directed relation can have one target, many targets, a target set, or several simultaneously tracked relations.

The identity problem concerns selection **among causal continuities through a history**, not merely directedness toward something. If “selection” is defined strongly enough to mean “choose which causal continuity counts for identity,” then that conclusion has been inserted into the meaning of reaching.

**Result:** semantic loading does not supply the bridge.

## 5. Test J3-3 — Finitude as the missing premise

The strongest repair is:

\[
I\text{ is finite}\land I\text{ reaches}
\Rightarrow
I\text{ cannot preserve every causal relation with equal practical salience}.
\]

This may be independently plausible, but it is stronger than Joint 3. And even bounded capacity does not entail exactly one selected thread: a finite subject can track several relations, alternate among them, or use different relations for different purposes.

**Result:** finitude is a plausible auxiliary premise, not a derivation from Joint 3. At most it supports **bounded selection**, not yet **unique identity selection**.

## 6. Test J3-4 — Purpose and interest

Interest-relative identity requires more than bounded attention. It requires that the mode of tracking be organized by some purpose or interest: preservation, function, provenance, responsibility, engineering continuity, psychological continuity, biological lineage, and so on.

Nothing in `I cannot not reach` alone establishes that every act of reaching is purpose-organized in this sense. A subject may reach experimentally, habitually, playfully, or without an explicitly represented purpose.

**Result:** interest is a plausible selection layer, but Joint 3 does not yet entail its presence in every act of reaching.

## 7. Test J3-5 — Strong countermodel

Construct a subject satisfying Joint 3 while:

1. necessarily reaching;
2. representing the complete causal history;
3. preserving process and material continuity simultaneously;
4. refusing to select either as *the* identity-bearing relation;
5. answering different questions by reporting different continuities.

Nothing in this construction contradicts `I cannot not reach`.

It directly defeats the strong inference from Joint 3 to unique identity-thread selection while leaving the weaker fact of directed reaching intact.

**Result:** the strong bridge fails as an entailment; the weaker tracking thesis remains live.

## 8. The auxiliary principle now exposed

The investigation may need an independently stated principle of the form:

> **Bounded Tracking Principle (candidate):** A finite reaching subject necessarily operates under some bounded mode of practical or epistemic tracking, and legitimate purposes can select different causal relations from a history.

This is a **new proposition**, not a restatement of Joint 3.

It must be attacked independently for circularity, arbitrary subjectivism, overgeneration, perfect-counterfeit preservation, fission/fusion, disagreement between subjects with identical purposes, cases without explicit purpose, and simultaneous multiple interests.

No candidate principle is promoted merely because it repairs the bridge.

## 9. What this does to the category-error claim

The Ship-of-Theseus diagnosis remains **Open**.

Two claims must now be kept separate:

**A — causal underdetermination:** physical history can contain multiple legitimate causal continuities without a physical fact selecting one as *the* identity-bearing continuity. This is strongly supported by the Theseus battery.

**B — subject-relative selection:** a reaching subject necessarily supplies the missing selection through an interest or purpose. This remains open and requires an independent bridge principle.

A does not automatically establish B.

### Locked next pressure

Do not upgrade Joint 3. Do not construct `G*_2`. Do not declare the category-error diagnosis established.

The next pressure is:

\[
\boxed{\text{finite reaching subject}\rightarrow\text{bounded tracking}\rightarrow\text{interest-relative causal selection}}
\]

The investigation must determine whether this chain can be specified without circularity and whether it can explain Theseus while preserving the causal constraints already earned by UCL.

## 11. Provisional conclusion

Joint 3 does not contain the whole solution. That is a sharpening, not a defeat.

The actual question beneath the original question is now:

> **Does being a finite reaching subject necessarily entail operating under a bounded mode of causal tracking, and can such a mode legitimately determine which continuity is relevant to an identity attribution without becoming arbitrary or circular?**

Until answered:

\[
\boxed{\text{Ship of Theseus exposes causal underdetermination; interest-relative selection is a live hypothesis, not yet a result.}}
\]
