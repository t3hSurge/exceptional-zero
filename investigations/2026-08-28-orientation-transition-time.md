# Distinction, Orientation, Transition, Time

**Status:** Open investigation  
**Scope:** Whether `A → B` can be specified without presupposing time, and whether any middle notion of transition is stronger than orientation and weaker than temporal succession  
**Depends on:** Actualization Boundary (regulative); Cut as distinction, not movement  
**Not in scope:** movers, time primitives, `C_*`, `G*_2`, `Σ`, identity ranking

---

## 1. Why this is upstream of identity

The diachronic-identity work asks which causal continuity counts *through* time. That question presupposes succession. The Cut, as earned in the canonical framework, supplies distinction, not movement:

$$
\mathrm{Cut}\Rightarrow\mathrm{distinction}
\qquad
\mathrm{Cut}\nRightarrow\mathrm{movement}
$$

$$
\mathrm{distinction}\nRightarrow\mathrm{succession}
$$

This is the Actualization Boundary applied to becoming: a structural condition can make something possible without making its occurrence necessary. `A | B` does not entail `A → B`.

The present investigation does not treat the Cut as time, and does not treat Aristotle's unmoved mover as an answer. The comparison is only that differentiated potentiality does not exhaust actuality/change.

Identity, `C_*`, and `G*` remain downstream. They are not revised here.

---

## 2. Austere question

$$
\boxed{\text{Can }A\rightarrow B\text{ be specified without presupposing time?}}
$$

Forbidden as primitives: time, earlier/later, before/after, duration, clock, succession, change, becoming, persistence, temporal "from…to," and causal precedence if that already means temporally prior.

Three readings must be kept apart:

1. **Temporal primitive.** `A → B` already means `A \prec_t B`. Time was in the description.
2. **Atemporal directed relation.** Some `R(A,B)` is independently specifiable and does not mean temporal order. Then: what turns `R` into an actual transition?
3. **Hidden temporal vocabulary.** The word "time" is avoided while succession, change, becoming, replacement, or equivalent content is smuggled back in.

Locked distinctions:

$$
\boxed{\text{orientation}\neq\text{transition}}
\qquad
\boxed{\text{transition}\neq\text{time}}
$$

until those bridges are earned.

## 3. Austere specification battery

### T1. Oriented edge

Let `G=(V,E)` with `E \subseteq V \times V`. Write `A → B` for `(A,B) \in E`.

This specifies orientation: an ordered pair. It does not say that `A` ceases, that `B` occurs, or that anything happens. Two co-present nodes plus an arrow are a static fact about a graph.

**Result:** atemporal specification is possible *for orientation*. Transition has not been introduced.

### T2. Functional dependence

`B = f(A)` is atemporal as mathematics. A lookup table is not a happening.

**Result:** functional direction `≠` transition.

### T3. Partial order

A poset can be specified without the word "time." Interpreting `≪` as "is succeeded by" reinserts transitional content by reading, not by the axioms.

**Result:** order can be atemporal as syntax; successor-as-happening has not been specified.

### T4. Hidden-vocabulary check

If the definition of `A → B` still needs succession, change, becoming, persistence, process, "then," "next," or "from…into" in order to mean more than an ordered pair, the account is reading 3.

### T5. Consequence

$$
\boxed{R(A,B)\text{ can be atemporal}}
$$

$$
\boxed{R(A,B)\text{ is not yet a transition}}
$$

Chain so far:

$$
\mathrm{Cut}\rightarrow\mathrm{distinction}
\qquad
\mathrm{distinction}\nRightarrow\mathrm{orientation}
\qquad
\mathrm{orientation}\nRightarrow\mathrm{transition}
$$

The Actualization Boundary still blocks:

$$
\text{possible }R(A,B)\nRightarrow\text{actual transition}
$$

## 4. Middle-definition battery

Live question after T1–T5:

> Can transition be defined as something stronger than an oriented relation and weaker than temporal succession, without hiding temporal content inside "occurrence," "actualization," "replacement," or an equivalent?

A candidate `T(A,B)` must be: (i) stronger than orientation; (ii) weaker than `A \prec_t B`; (iii) free of hidden temporal vocabulary; (iv) able to distinguish a mere admissible pair from a transition.

### M1. Mutual exclusion of co-actuality

`A` and `B` cannot both be actual. Too weak: incompatibility is not becoming.

$$
\boxed{\text{incompatibility}\neq\text{transition}}
$$

### M2. Exclusive actuality of exactly one

Exactly one of `{A,B}` is actual. Still static: a world-inventory can contain one actual state and one non-actual alternative. Direction is also missing.

### M3. Replacement

`B` replaces `A`. Stronger than an edge, but "replace" is the target unless independently defined. "No longer actual" is temporal.

**Fails hidden-vocabulary check.**

### M4. Actualization / realization

`B` is the actualization of what `A` made possible. This is the Aristotelian slot. Using "actualization" as the definition of transition would turn the Actualization Boundary from a warning into a primitive of becoming. Not derived from the Cut; not a stripped definition.

### M5. World-index shift

Actuality "moves" from `w_A` to `w_B`. "Moves" is the target. A directed relation on worlds is orientation at a higher type. A time-index presupposes time.

### M6. Asymmetric dependence of actuality

The actuality of `B` depends on the actuality of `A`, not conversely. This can be wholly atemporal (grounding, mathematical determination). Dependence is not passage. If "depends" is specified as causal production, temporal priority typically re-enters.

**Can be atemporal; does not yet give transition.**

### M7. Joint 3 as the extra fact

A subject cannot not reach across the pair. Off-target: this may describe the phenomenology of undergoing a transition already granted. It does not define transition in the stripped structural vocabulary, and by earlier work it does not select `A → B` rather than some other pair.

## 5. Partition of the search space

Tested candidates fell into three bins:

$$
\boxed{\text{static structure}}
\quad\big|\quad
\boxed{\text{hidden succession}}
\quad\big|\quad
\boxed{\text{off-target phenomenology}}
$$

M2 and M6 are the instructive pair: exclusive actuality can be static; asymmetric dependence can be atemporal. Neither is passage.

Earned search result:

$$
\boxed{\text{no tested middle definition of transition survives austerity}}
$$

Not earned:

$$
\boxed{\text{no such definition is possible}}
$$

Status wording, with "not yet" explicit:

$$
\boxed{\text{transition is not yet a third kind of fact in the stripped vocabulary}}
$$

This is not ontological evidence that no such fact exists.

## 6. Live existence question

$$
\boxed{
\exists T\;[\,T>\text{orientation}\;\land\;T<\text{temporal succession}\;\land\;T\text{ is not temporal in disguise}\,]\;?
}
$$

If a later exhaustion argument answered this with no, then explaining time by appealing to transition would be backwards: transition would already contain the temporal structure under investigation. That conditional is **not earned**. It is only what a negative answer would mean.

What is earned:

$$
\boxed{\text{orientation}\nRightarrow\text{transition}}
$$

Every tested thickening was either still static or already succession.
