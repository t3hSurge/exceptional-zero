## 11. Isolated missing object

Admissibility is not enough. Orientation is not enough. Execution order is not enough. A complete set of possible histories is not enough.

What is missing is specifically:

$$
\boxed{\text{actuality-selection}}
$$

without simply stipulating actuality.

Four attack families remain distinct:

$$
\text{constraint}\;|\;\text{selection}\;|\;\text{instantiation}\;|\;\text{primitive fact}
$$

Constraint is already eliminated as a sufficient account:

$$
\text{admissible}\nRightarrow\text{actual}
$$

Pressure now falls on selection and instantiation. They are not assumed identical.

- **Selection** asks what picks one admissible possibility rather than another.
- **Instantiation** asks what changes the ontological status of a possibility from merely possible to actual.

A selector could choose among already-existing actual candidates. An instantiator could make one candidate actual in the first place.

Three-way separation, not to be collapsed:

$$
\boxed{\text{possible}}\neq\boxed{\text{selected}}\neq\boxed{\text{actual}}
$$

The Actualization Boundary specifically warns against collapsing the first two.
