# Joint 3 → History-Mediated Interest: Bridge Test

**Status:** Open investigation  
**Scope:** Whether prior reaching-history can supply propositional interest without presupposing diachronic identity  
**No `G*_2`. No `Σ`. No process/material preference.**

---

## 1. Why this test is separate

The preceding Joint 3 bridge test established only a non-entailment:

\[
J3=(I\text{ cannot not reach})\not\Rightarrow\text{unique causal-thread selection}.
\]

A stronger objection was then identified: this does not exhaust the possibility that **temporally extended history** supplies the missing content. Bare, pre-propositional reaching may be content-free at an instant while a history of reaching contributes to later interest formation.

This investigation therefore tests BRI-3 option B directly rather than treating it as closed.

---

## 2. Candidate bridge

The candidate is not:

\[
J3\rightarrow I.
\]

It is instead something like:

\[
J3 + H_R + L \rightarrow I,
\]

where:

- `H_R` is an independently specified history of prior reaching;
- `L` is a learning, preference-update, or disposition-forming mechanism;
- `I` is a present propositional interest.

The test asks whether `H_R` can be defined without identity language and whether it can determine `I` without arbitrary supplementation.

---

## 3. B-1 — Personal-history formulation

The obvious formulation is:

> the history of what this self has reached toward.

This fails immediately if “this self” means the diachronically identical subject whose persistence is itself under investigation. Selecting which prior states belong to the present self would presuppose the target fact.

**Result:**

\[
\boxed{\text{Personal-history-as-identity-presupposed is circular.}}
\]

This is a genuine Construction-B-style failure.

---

## 4. B-2 — Causal-ancestry reformulation

A non-circular alternative is to define history structurally rather than personally.

For a present state `s_t`, let:

\[
A(s_t)=\{s_i:s_i\leadsto s_t\},
\]

where `\leadsto` is an independently specified causal-ancestry relation.

Then identify prior reaching events inside that ancestry:

\[
R_t=\{r_i\in A(s_t):r_i\text{ is a reaching event/state}\}.
\]

No diachronic identity predicate is required. The construction refers only to causal ancestry of the present state.

**Result:**

\[
\boxed{\text{Causal ancestry can, in principle, provide a non-identity-bearing history object.}}
\]

This is an important correction to the earlier pressure: option B is not exhausted merely by noting that a personal-history formulation is circular.

---

## 5. B-3 — Does causal ancestry itself generate interest?

The answer is no, not by itself.

A causal ancestry may contain many reaching events with different contents:

\[
r_1,r_2,\ldots,r_n.
\]

The existence of that set does not uniquely determine which present proposition is of interest.

A further mechanism is required, schematically:

\[
L(H_R)\rightarrow I_t.
\]

Possible forms include learning, reinforcement, preference updating, habit formation, or another independently specified causal update rule.

**Result:**

\[
\boxed{H_R\text{ can be an input to interest formation, but does not itself determine }I_t.}}
\]

Therefore the bridge remains unearned unless an additional content-generating mechanism is supplied.

---

## 6. B-4 — Can the learning mechanism be independently specified?

In principle, yes. A theory may specify a causal mechanism by which prior states alter present dispositions or preferences without mentioning diachronic identity.

That would yield a genuine extended construction:

\[
J3 + H_R + L \rightarrow I.
\]

But this is a new auxiliary theory, not a consequence of Joint 3.

Its legitimacy therefore depends on independent tests of `L`, including representation invariance, non-arbitrariness, multiple interests, conflicting histories, cases without explicit purposes, and whether the mechanism merely reproduces a desired identity verdict.

No such `L` is promoted here.

**Result:** history-mediated interest formation is **live as an auxiliary hypothesis**, not established as a theorem of Joint 3.

---

## 7. B-5 — Theseus discrimination

Return to the de-loaded replacement/recombination construction, whose causal graph contains at least:

\[
P=\text{process continuity}
\]

and

\[
M=\text{material continuity}.
\]

A history-dependent mechanism could produce:

\[
I_P,\qquad I_M,\qquad I_{P+M},
\]

depending on the subject's causal-developmental history.

This shows that history-mediated interest can explain why different subjects may acquire different tracking priorities.

It does **not** establish:

\[
I_P\Rightarrow P\text{ is identity},
\]

or

\[
I_M\Rightarrow M\text{ is identity}.
\]

Thus:

\[
\boxed{\text{history can explain interest plurality without yet establishing identity selection.}}
\]

---

## 8. B-6 — Second-order circularity check

The causal-ancestry construction must remain strictly distinct from:

> the mechanism preserves the interests of the same subject across time.

That formulation reintroduces the identity target.

The admissible form is instead:

\[
s_i\leadsto s_t,
\]

not:

\[
s_i=\text{the same person as }s_t.
\]

The former is structural ancestry; the latter is the disputed identity relation.

**Result:** the auxiliary construction remains non-circular only if it stays entirely on the causal-ancestry side of this distinction.

---

## 9. B-7 — Determinacy pressure

Even with an independently specified learning mechanism, multiple interests may coexist:

\[
I_P, I_M, I_F, I_{info},\ldots
\]

A history-mediated mechanism therefore need not yield a unique identity-relevant relation.

That is not automatically a failure of interest formation. It means the mechanism explains the generation of practical/epistemic interests rather than automatically converting one interest into an identity criterion.

Any stronger claim requires another bridge:

\[
I\rightarrow C_*.
\]

That bridge remains separately open.

---

## 10. Corrected result

The strict bridge:

\[
J3\rightarrow I
\]

**fails as presently specified.** Bare, pre-propositional reaching does not contain enough content to derive a particular propositional interest.

The temporally extended construction is different:

\[
\boxed{J3 + H_R + L \rightarrow I}
\]

is **coherent as an auxiliary hypothesis**, provided `H_R` is defined by causal ancestry rather than identity and `L` is independently specified.

No such `L` has yet been constructed or promoted.

Therefore the result is not “the bridge is closed across all historical versions.” It is:

\[
\boxed{\text{Bare J3 does not entail interest; history-mediated interest formation remains live.}}
\]

---

## 11. Relation to interest-relative identity

This test does not establish:

\[
\text{Diachronic identity} = \text{interest-relative causality}.
\]

It establishes only that a plausible route from reaching to interest requires additional temporal and causal machinery.

The remaining architecture is therefore:

\[
J3
\;\not\Rightarrow\;
I
\]

but potentially:

\[
J3+H_R+L\rightarrow I
\;\;?\;\;
I\rightarrow C_*
\;\;?\;\;
C_*\rightarrow\text{identity attribution}.
\]

Each `?` is an independent burden.

## 13. Locked next pressure

Do not upgrade Joint 3. Do not treat the auxiliary history-mediated mechanism as established. Do not construct `G*_2` merely to continue the prior selective-candidate sequence.

The subsequent ownership-filter investigation showed that unrestricted causal ancestry is not yet a subject-specific `H_R`. The required restriction `R_t^* \subseteq A(s_t)` remains independently unspecified. Therefore the dependency is:

\[
J3 \;\rightarrow\; A(s_t) \;\rightarrow\; R_t^* \;\rightarrow\; L \;\rightarrow\; I
\]

not merely `J3 + H_R + L \rightarrow I` with `H_R` already in hand.

If this line is continued, the next legitimate test is the ownership filter recorded in `investigations/2026-08-23-joint3-ownership-filter-pressure.md`, not independent specification of `L`. `L` cannot be meaningfully instantiated until `R_t^*` is independently specified.

The separate bridge:

\[
I\rightarrow C_*
\]

must remain distinct from that investigation.
