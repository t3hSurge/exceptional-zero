# AIPC 2026 — epistemic method

This document records the epistemic discipline the competition workflow is intended to expose and report. It is not a theory that the submitted essays must endorse.

## 1. Core rule

> **Nothing is stronger than its status.**

A proposition should not acquire stronger warrant merely because it is elegant, repeated, intuitively compelling, or useful for generating further questions.

## 2. Separate the layers

Every investigation should distinguish at least:

1. **Question** — what is being asked.
2. **Candidate** — a proposed answer, definition, bridge, or construction.
3. **Argument** — what is offered in support of the candidate.
4. **Objection** — what would defeat, weaken, or redirect the argument.
5. **Result** — what the investigation actually establishes.
6. **Status** — the epistemic standing of that result.

Do not collapse these layers into one another.

## 3. Status vocabulary

### Derived

A claim follows under stated assumptions or from an explicit argument.

### Believed

A claim is currently endorsed but has not been established by the available argument.

### Boundary

A tested route has failed, or a distinction has been established that blocks an inference.

A Boundary is not a disguised theorem of impossibility.

### Open

The question remains live. Existing arguments do not settle it.

### Regulative Principle

A methodological rule retained because it prevents an identified inference error. It is not thereby an ontological fact.

## 4. Negative results are first-class results

A failed candidate should be recorded with enough precision to answer:

- What exactly was attempted?
- What would have counted as success?
- Where did it fail?
- Was the failure logical, semantic, methodological, or merely evidential?
- What weaker residue survives?

Do not replace a failed bridge with a thicker synonym and call the bridge repaired.

## 5. No smuggled primitives

A concept may not do explanatory work merely because its ordinary-language meaning already contains the missing phenomenon.

Watch especially for:

- time smuggled through “before,” “after,” or “becoming”;
- occurrence smuggled through “actual,” “happens,” or “performance”;
- causation smuggled through “produces” or “makes happen”;
- agency smuggled through “does” or “acts”;
- unity smuggled through “whole” or “everything”;
- identity smuggled through “the same” without an identity criterion.

When a candidate requires one of these concepts, specify the concept independently or mark the dependency as open.

## 6. Structural versus actual

A formal relation, transformation, ordering, or recurrence does not by itself establish that anything is instantiated, occurring, performed, or temporally unfolding.

Likewise:

`description ≠ instantiation`

`formal succession ≠ temporal succession`

`structural change ≠ actual occurrence`

These distinctions are methodological safeguards, not conclusions about what reality ultimately is.

## 7. Evaluation without replacement

Adversarial evaluation should identify defects without automatically supplying the missing argument.

A clean evaluation may conclude:

> The candidate does not establish its conclusion; the missing bridge has not been specified.

That is preferable to inventing a new premise merely to preserve the candidate.

When a substantive replacement is generated, record it as a new candidate rather than silently modifying the old one.

## 8. Independence of generation

For the competition essay, the human may select the question and establish generic constraints, but the substantive argument should come from the AI generation process.

Therefore the generation record should make it possible to distinguish:

- ideas present in the prompt;
- ideas generated by the AI;
- generic human corrections;
- substantive human contributions, if any;
- later evaluator discoveries.

The last category must not be retroactively represented as part of the original generation.

## 9. Reopening is permitted

A later AI instance may challenge an earlier result.

It should not be required to accept an earlier conclusion merely because it is recorded in the repository. The repository records the state of inquiry; it does not become an oracle.

If a later instance overturns a prior result, preserve both the old result and the reason for revision.

## 10. Epistemic portability

The point of codifying the method is that another model should be able to reproduce the *discipline* without inheriting the *answer*.

A fresh instance should be able to know:

- what counts as a claim;
- what counts as support;
- what counts as a failure;
- what distinctions must not be collapsed;
- what status labels mean;
- when to stop rather than invent a bridge;
- how to record uncertainty.

It should not need to be told what conclusion to reach.
