**Status of review:** Complete for the current round

## 1. Executive finding

Track 2 is doing the right job: it searches for a single clear survivor that would falsify any claim that the recurring signature is exceptionless.

The four candidates recorded so far do not survive. However, the review reveals an important difference among their failures:

- Candidate A is a failed reduction from identity to continuity/information, with a useful but overly strong probabilistic clause.
- Candidate B restates the correspondence problem at the level of the interpretation function.
- Candidate D fails even before the circularity question is interesting: non-transitivity of a substructure does not uniquely select a genuine chain and can be trivially produced by ordinary asymmetry within a chain.
- Candidate E confuses preservation of particularizing distinctions with truth and inherits the mapping problem.

The search therefore has four genuine failures, but they should not be treated as four equally deep impossibility results. Some candidates fail because their criterion is simply too weak; others fail because the proposed criterion is target-dependent or mis-scoped.

**Track 2 remains Open.**

---

## 2. Candidate A — causal-informational continuity with “measure-zero” branching discrimination

The proposed relation requires a continuous causal chain plus the condition that any alternative chain producing the same information at the present state is “of measure zero” or excluded by physical law.

The continuity part is independently specified. The trouble begins with the second condition.

### 2.1 “Measure zero” does not solve identity

A measure-zero alternative is still an alternative.

A set can have probability zero while containing an actually realized point. More importantly, identity is not a probability question. The target is not:

> Which candidate is overwhelmingly likely to be the same self?

It is:

> Which candidate is the same self, if either?

So measure-theoretic rarity cannot by itself turn continuity into identity.

### 2.2 “Excluded by the physical laws” is potentially target-bearing

If the laws of the world genuinely forbid a branching or counterfeit history, that could be an independent physical constraint. But the proposal must specify those laws without using identity to determine which histories count as alternative candidates.

Otherwise “the laws exclude the alternative” risks becoming another version of:

> the genuine history is the one the laws permit as genuine.

That would require a separate formal model, not an appeal to physical-law language.

### 2.3 What survives

The useful core is modest:

\[
\text{identity may require more than causal continuity and informational continuity.}
\]

That is a real negative result, but it does not point toward a survivor.

**Verdict: failed candidate.**

## 3. Candidate B — primitive correspondence under a fixed interpretation function

This candidate is essentially a mapping theory with the mapping elevated to a primitive.

It says, in effect:

\[
r\text{ is true}\iff I(r)\text{ lands on an obtaining fact}.
\]

The problem is not that an interpretation function cannot be mathematically introduced. Of course it can.

The problem is that the proposed function must do the semantic work that the earlier correspondence attempts needed the “right mapping” to do.

If \(I\) is stipulated so that the right statement maps to the right fact, the problem has been renamed rather than solved.

If \(I\) is fixed by community convention, convention explains shared usage but does not by itself explain why the mapping is truth-preserving.

If \(I\) is fixed by causal history, then the account risks reducing to the tracking/provenance machinery already tested elsewhere.

So Candidate B does not survive the privileged-mapping pressure test.

**Verdict: failed candidate.**

---

## 4. Candidate D — the chain that forces Z

This is the most important failed candidate in Track 2 because it attempts to use the formal Exceptional Zero directly rather than merely invoking ordinary continuity.

The proposed idea is to select the unique subset \(G\) whose induced structure is minimal and whose Z-status is not shared by the counterfeit.

The problem is deeper than the explicit circularity already identified.

### 4.1 Z is not a selector unless the asymmetry is already present

Suppose the genuine chain and counterfeit are perfectly relationally isomorphic under the permitted vocabulary.

Then no automorphism-invariant fact separates them.

So asking which chain “forces Z” cannot produce a distinction that was absent from the combined structure.

Z reports non-transitivity. It does not manufacture non-transitivity between two candidates that were deliberately constructed to remain in the same orbit.

### 4.2 Z of a substructure is not automatically a personal-identity criterion

There is a second problem worth adding to the existing investigation.

An ordinary finite temporal chain can itself have a trivial automorphism group. In a linearly ordered structure, for example, the endpoints may already be structurally distinguished. Non-transitivity of the induced substructure is therefore not special to “genuine history.” It can arise from ordinary internal shape.

So even if a candidate biography has \(Z(G)\), that fact does not tell us that the corresponding chain is a self. It may merely tell us that the chain is asymmetric in some ordinary structural respect.

The proposed “minimal Z-forcing chain” therefore lacks uniqueness even before the counterfeit case is considered.

### 4.3 Result

Candidate D does not merely fail to derive identity. Its selection criterion is not yet a well-formed identity criterion.

**Verdict: failed candidate, with an additional structural objection.**

---

## 5. Candidate E — truth as preservation of Z-forcing distinctions

Candidate E says roughly that a representation is true when it preserves a distinction responsible for intrinsic particularity.

There are two independent problems.

### 5.1 Coverage

Truth is not restricted to propositions about exceptional particularity.

A true statement can concern an ordinary relation in a structure that creates no new Z and does not preserve any particularizing distinction in the candidate sense.

So the criterion is too narrow to cover truth as ordinarily intended.

### 5.2 Mapping

Even where the proposition does concern a structural asymmetry, the account still needs to identify which distinction the representation preserves and why that is the relevant actual distinction rather than an isomorphic alternative.

That is the old mapping problem in new notation.

The proposal therefore does not escape correspondence; it selects a special class of facts to which correspondence is supposed to apply and leaves the “which one?” problem intact.

**Verdict: failed candidate.**

---

## 6. The most useful conclusion from the four failures

Track 2 should not summarize the result merely as:

> Four candidates failed.

The stronger methodological result is:

> The candidates fail in different ways, but every attempted reduction so far either substitutes a relation for identity/truth/for-this without earning the substitution, imports the target while pretending to explain it, or shifts from the target to an adjacent structural property.

That matters because it prevents the failures from being overcounted. They are not four independent proofs of impossibility.

They are four differently diagnosed demonstrations of the same demand:

\[
\boxed{\text{A candidate must be independently specified before it can select the target.}}
\]

That is the real survivor-search result so far.

## 7. Pressure test for the proposed next candidates

Track 2 currently proposes three directions: derived resources already accepted by the framework, negative characterization, and continued candidate generation.

### 7.1 Reusing derived resources

This is worth doing, but the test must distinguish “derived resource” from “target-bearing resource.”

The Cut gives:

\[
\text{particularity}\Rightarrow Z.
\]

It does not independently give a relation saying which of several symmetric candidates is the actual particular one.

The Actualization Boundary is a regulative principle, not an object-level mechanism, so it cannot be used to manufacture the missing asymmetry.

Thus a candidate built only from these resources faces a strong prior: it must introduce some genuinely new structure if it is to break a symmetry that the existing resources themselves do not break.

### 7.2 Negative characterization

The proposed candidate is roughly:

> choose the chain that is not a perfect relational copy of another.

This fails immediately in the explicit two-copy model because both chains are copies. If exactly one is to count as original, that fact must come from an additional relation not contained in the copy vocabulary.

A negative criterion cannot distinguish two candidates that satisfy the negative condition equally.

So this particular route is blocked by the existing counterfeit construction.

### 7.3 Continued candidate generation

This is still necessary. A single survivor would be decisive evidence against an exceptionless recurring signature.

But future candidates should be organized by their proposed source of asymmetry, not merely by philosophical school names. The important question is:

> **What independently specified fact does this candidate introduce that the counterfeit cannot copy?**

If the answer is “the candidate is genuinely mine,” “the real history,” “the actual interpretation,” or an equivalent phrase, the candidate fails the anti-circularity rule immediately.

## 8. Recommended survivor-search taxonomy

Future candidates should be sorted into families before testing:

1. **Continuity candidates** — causal, psychological, bodily, functional, informational.
2. **Historical-origin candidates** — provenance, ancestry, unique causal origin, event-relative relations.
3. **Counterfactual candidates** — what would have happened under specified interventions.
4. **Indexical/first-person candidates** — ownership, authority, privileged access, self-reference.
5. **Structural-invariant candidates** — properties preserved under a specified class of transformations.
6. **Primitive candidates** — explicitly refuse reduction and ask whether the remaining primitive can be constrained without becoming vacuous.

The classification is methodological, not ontological. It prevents the search from repeatedly proposing cosmetic variants of the same relation.

Each candidate must then answer three questions in this order:

1. What is the relation without mentioning the target?
2. What structure would it generate in the P/R counterfeit case?
3. Does it distinguish the target, and if so, what non-target fact supplies the asymmetry?

Only the third question should decide whether it survives.

---

## 9. Status recommendation

**Track 2 remains Open.**

The four recorded failures should remain in the investigation because their failure modes are informative, but none warrants a stronger universal conclusion.

The strongest current search result is:

> No independently specified survivor has yet been found among the candidate families actually tested; the failures so far are diagnosable as underdetermination, circularity, target-shift, or insufficient coverage.

That is evidence against the currently tested family of approaches. It is not evidence that the survivor space is empty.

The next search should therefore prioritize genuinely new sources of asymmetry rather than more elaborate versions of continuity, correspondence, or “Z selects the real one.”
