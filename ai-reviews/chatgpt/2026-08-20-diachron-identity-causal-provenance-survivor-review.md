# Review: Diachronic Identity — Causal Provenance / Unique-Origin Survivor

**Date:** 2026-08-20  
**Reviewed investigation:** `investigations/2026-08-20-diachron-identity-causal-provenance-survivor.md`  
**Status:** Complete for the current round  
**Framework status affected:** Recurring Signature remains Open; Diachronic Identity remains a Boundary scoped to previously tested relational vocabulary.

---

## 1. Executive finding

A genuine new survivor has been found, but it needs to be stated more narrowly than “causal provenance explains identity.”

The strongest candidate is **Unique Causal Lineage (UCL)**: diachronic identity is assigned to stages belonging to the same uniquely originated, non-branching causal lineage, where the origin condition is independently specified and the causal relations are third-person structural facts.

Under the current battery, UCL survives the principal counterexamples that defeated ordinary continuity and memory accounts. Most importantly, it survives the implanted-memory/perfect-counterfeit test **by introducing an independently specified external causal relation rather than by inserting identity into the structure**.

That is a real counterexample to the broad form of the recurring-signature hypothesis.

It is not yet a proof that UCL is correct.

---

## 2. Why this is different from the earlier causal-continuity candidate

Simple causal continuity says roughly:

\[
y\to x \Rightarrow I(y,x).
\]

That fails under branching because one predecessor can have two equally continuous successors.

UCL adds a structural constraint:

\[
\text{unique origin} + \text{causal provenance} + \text{non-branching continuation}.
\]

The difference matters. UCL is not merely “more causal continuity”; it changes what counts as a persistence path.

---

## 3. Ship of Theseus

Gradual replacement preserves a single causal lineage, so UCL preserves identity through the replacement sequence.

The later reconstruction from the original removed parts has a distinct causal history and is therefore not identical under UCL.

This yields a definite answer without material sameness.

The test is passed.

---

## 4. Duplication and fission

For

\[
A\to\{B,C\},
\]

mere ancestry is insufficient. UCL instead treats the branching point as the end of the pre-branch identity lineage.

Thus neither successor is identical to the predecessor across the split.

This is a substantive metaphysical cost, but not a formal defect. It respects the ordinary transitivity constraint on numerical identity and avoids the impossible result that one thing is identical with two numerically distinct successors.

The candidate therefore survives the fission test, while making a clear prediction: **branching terminates diachronic identity**.

---

## 5. Convergence

For

\[
A\to C,
\qquad
B\to C,
\]

UCL cannot identify C with both prior lineages. Convergence therefore terminates the prior identities or produces a newly individuated state.

Again, this follows from the independent lineage rule rather than from the target term “same self.”

The test is passed.

---

## 6. Memory and discontinuity

Memory is not constitutive of UCL identity. Memory loss therefore does not destroy identity, and implanted memory does not create it.

Likewise, a temporal gap does not matter so long as the causal lineage remains intact and non-branching.

This is a genuine improvement over memory-based accounts.

---

## 7. The decisive counterfeit test

The earlier counterfeit construction makes genuine and rival histories identical with respect to temporal, internal causal, memory, and informational relations.

UCL can distinguish them only if the permitted structure includes an independently specified causal-provenance relation connecting one chain to a distinct origin/environmental history.

Then:

\[
P\not\equiv_{UCL}R
\]

without adding a predicate such as “same-self” or “actual past.”

This is exactly the form of counterexample the earlier recurring-signature search was looking for.

It shows that **external causal embedding can supply asymmetry without using identity as the source of that asymmetry**.

That defeats any universal claim that every non-target-bearing third-person vocabulary must collapse under the perfect-counterfeit construction.

---

## 8. The remaining vulnerability: origin selection

The candidate is not automatically secure merely because “origin” sounds objective.

The decisive question is whether the origin predicate can be independently specified.

Bad formulation:

> the origin of the genuine self.

That is Construction B in disguise.

Acceptable formulation:

> a physically or historically specified event satisfying a criterion stated without reference to personal identity.

For example, a defined creation event or another independently specified causal boundary could serve as the origin in a particular domain.

The general theory therefore still has work to do: it must explain why one origin predicate is principled rather than merely tailored to the entities under discussion.

That is the surviving challenge.

---

## 9. What the survivor establishes about Track 1

Track 1's **conditional counterfeit theorem remains completely intact**:

> if two candidates are exact relational copies under the whole permitted signature, automorphism invariance prevents intrinsic distinction.

What fails is the attempted universalization that every admissible third-person account can be forced into that copyable class.

UCL supplies a concrete reason why that may fail: some accounts may legitimately depend on external provenance relations that the counterfeit does not share.

So the correct update is:

\[
\text{perfect-copy symmetry theorem: intact}
\]

but

\[
\text{“all non-target third-person accounts are copyable”: refuted as a universal candidate}
\]

at least unless that account is given a narrower formal class that explicitly excludes causal provenance.

---

## 10. What the survivor establishes about the recurring signature

The broad claim:

> every third-person account tested fails in the recurring way

is no longer tenable.

A more defensible statement is:

> **The initial internal-relational vocabularies repeatedly failed; an externally anchored causal-provenance account has now produced a survivor under the current test battery.**

That is a substantive change in the research state.

The recurring-signature investigation should remain **Open**, but its next purpose is no longer simply to search for the first survivor. It now needs to determine whether UCL is genuinely explanatory or merely relocates the selection problem into the definition of “origin.”

---

## 11. Status

**UCL: provisional survivor.**

**Diachronic Identity:** retains its existing Boundary status, but the boundary must remain explicitly scoped to the relational vocabulary previously exhausted.

**Recurring Signature:** remains Open, with its universal/exceptionless form now defeated by at least one independently specified candidate.

The correct next pressure test is not another generic continuity case. It is:

> **Can the origin condition be independently specified and generalized without covertly reintroducing the identity it is meant to explain?**

If yes, UCL becomes a serious reductive theory. If no, the survivor collapses back into Construction B under a more sophisticated name.
